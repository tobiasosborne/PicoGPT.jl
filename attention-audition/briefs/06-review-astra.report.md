# Mathematical review of Chapter 5

Line numbers refer to the audition chapter unless another file is named. Findings are ordered by their effect on the argument.

1. **Error — Exercise 5.4, line 142. The affine decoder needs the original bits.** The identity for $ab$ holds on all four inputs. The claimed universality does not follow for an affine map of $o$ alone, or even of $(b,o)$ if the reader retains its own bit $b$. For example, prescribe the target log-odds $L(a,b)=ab$. At $b=0$, the two distinct values $o=0,1/2$ must give the same log-odds. This forces the coefficient of $o$ to vanish. At $b=1$, the log-odds must then take both 0 and 1. Replace the last instruction with “Give the decoder the three features $(a,b,o(a,b))$. Write the four target log-odds as $c_0+c_1a+c_2b+c_3ab$, substitute the identity and apply the binary Gibbs readout.” Specify $0<P(Y=1\mid a,b)<1$ for each pair. This yields the intended constructive proof.

2. **Error — Exercise 5.3, line 138. The permutation symmetry is a first-block result.** With $P=0$ in the first block, permuting the preceding characters permutes their key/value pairs together, leaves the reader fixed and preserves the weighted sum. In the second block, the source at position 1 has seen one token and the source at position 2 has seen two. Swapping the raw tokens need no longer permute their incoming columns. As a direct check, use the specimen parameters, omit $P$ and apply the same trained block twice. For the prefixes $\mathtt{bot}$ and $\mathtt{obt}$, the first coordinate of head 1's output at reader 3 is $0.158889$ for both after the first attention operation. At the second it is $0.292221$ and $0.531188$, respectively. Ask “In a one-block model with $P=0$, which prefixes with the same last character must give the same prediction? Which part of your proof fails for a second causal block?” This corrects a scope error inherited from the old exercise.

3. **Error — Step 1 to Step 2, lines 31–37. The same symbol denotes an exact marginal and a candidate fit.** By construction $\widetilde P(y\mid c)=\sum_j\widetilde P(y,j\mid c)=P(y\mid c)$. Line 36 reuses that marginal for the restricted shared-rule mixture. The equality would assert that the new rules already fit the target. Write $p_{\rm mix}(y\mid c;\theta):=\sum_j\pi_\theta(j\mid c)g_{\theta,y}(x_i,v_j)$ and say that this family approximates $P$. The uniform-kernel example correctly shows why a simple selector alone buys nothing. Require simplicity of both factors **across prefixes**, with approximate fits allowed. Qualify the nonuniqueness by $i>1$, since the extension is forced when $i=1$. Even simple factors need not identify a true source. The interpretation of $J$ comes from assigning position $j$'s features to component $j$.

4. **Error — Score scale, lines 51–54 and Figure 5.2 caption, line 74. Score variance does not determine row entropy.** Under the stated independence assumptions the variance of a dot product really is $d$. But take all keys in a row to be the same random vector, with independent centred unit-variance coordinates independent of the query. Each score still has the stated variance and the softmax is uniform at every width and temperature. For $i>1$ the softmax Jacobian at that row is nonzero. Replace the freezing sentence with “Large gaps between the largest score and its competitors concentrate the row and make its softmax Jacobian small. Dividing by $\sqrt d$ sets the marginal score variance to one in this initialisation model.” For fixed energies the cold limit is uniform on the minimisers and the hot limit is uniform on all allowed sources. At positive finite temperature all allowed weights are positive. Both overstatements were present in the old chapter.

5. **Error — Steps 2–4, lines 35–49. Restrict the claims about which information and interactions are lost.** At a fixed reader, equal $v_j$ give equal component predictions. A feature removed by $W_v$ can still change $k_j$, the attention weight and the final prediction when other values differ. Replace “anything … erases is lost” with “the component decoder cannot distinguish such sources.” The score itself is affine in each input separately. After removing the row constant it retains a source-linear bias, so calling it a bilinear energy throughout also misses a real degree of freedom. One formula states exactly when the selector fits. For every allowed source pair $j,k$, the desired row must satisfy

   $$
   \log\frac{\pi_j}{\pi_k}
   =\frac{(W_q\hat x_i+b_q)^\top W_k(\hat x_j-\hat x_k)}{\sqrt d}.
   $$

   With shared factors, these odds are sufficient as well, since they determine each positive normalised row. The bilinear coefficient $B=W_q^\top W_k$ has **rank at most** $d$, and every matrix of that rank admits such a factorisation. A higher-rank coefficient cannot agree on the whole feature space, but can agree on the features actually visited. For example, when $\gamma_1=\mathbf1,\ \beta_1=0$, let $B$ project onto a $d$-dimensional subspace of $\mathbf1^\perp$, with $d<D$. Then $B+\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$ has rank $d+1$ and gives exactly the same centred-column scores as $B$. Scope the rank bound to the coefficient before softmax. The parameter counts $64$ versus $64$, plus eight offsets per head, are correct. The key offset cancels exactly, giving zero loss gradient.

6. **Error — Heads, lines 105–111. The displayed output has two heads in a formula for $H$ heads.** The displayed stack has $2d$ rows, while the formula declares $D=Hd$ rows. Write $O=\operatorname{vcat}(O^{(1)},\ldots,O^{(H)})$ or label the two-row display as the specimen. Replace “more heads consult more sources” with “at fixed $D$, more heads permit more distinct attention patterns, each with rank bound $d=D/H$.” One head already assigns positive mass to every allowed source, and several heads may learn identical patterns. The product law is a choice for the mixture experiment. All couplings with the same marginals give the displayed mean, although their decoded mixtures can differ.

7. **Gap — Mean-field step, lines 77–83. Give the real logit map and the scale of ‘first order’.** The normalised product identity and the equality criterion modulo a common logit shift are correct. Affine logits suffice. Even nonlinear logits $z(m)=Cm+b+h(m)\mathbf1$ satisfy the criterion exactly. For the one-block specimen, at fixed reader, write $u=x_i+W_om+b_o$, $w=u+W_2\operatorname{ReLU}(W_1\mathrm{LN}_2(u)+b_1)+b_2$, and $z(m)=W_{\rm out}\mathrm{LN}_f(w)+b_{\rm out}$. Here $m$ stacks the heads. At fixed $x_i$ the output projection and first residual addition are affine. LayerNorm generally remains nonlinear even with fixed ReLU gates. For values clustered in one smooth neighbourhood, bound the logit Hessians on the segments from the mean to the sources. Taylor's linear terms average to zero and bounded softmax derivative gives a probability discrepancy $O(s^2)$. Merely checking that none of the evaluated points is a kink is insufficient. For equally weighted $m=-t,2t$ and logits $(\max(0,m),0)$, every evaluated point, including the mean, is smooth for $t>0$, yet the first-logit discrepancy is $t/2$. State the same segment condition for the probability Hessian in line 77. Its near-copy estimate follows by comparing the mean's squared error with that of the dominant value as centre. For an intermediate block of a deeper model, a local two-argument readout to final logits would also need the other downstream attention inputs fixed.

9. **Gap — MPS aside, line 26. Distinguish the memory limit, cut rank and correlation length.** The specimen has a fixed context limit $T=8$. Its addressable storage grows with prefix length *up to* $T$. The displayed MPS factorisation bounds the rank of the amplitude table across that cut by $\chi$. Neither this rank nor the count of token columns in a supplied prefix establishes a correlation length. For example, the equal superposition of the two constant bit strings has bond dimension two and perfectly correlated distant bits. Replace the end of the aside with “The bond bounds the rank across a cut of the amplitude table. Attention stores up to $T$ separate columns and shares the addressing rule across them. Its head-width bound applies to a pair score.” If retaining a correlation claim, specify the absence of a uniform bound from this dimensional argument.
