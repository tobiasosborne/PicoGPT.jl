# When does a grown PicoGPT have its parent as a quotient?

**Scope.** Definitions specify the objects. Paragraphs labelled **Proved** include their argument or refer to an earlier proof. **Checked** refers to the finite witnesses in the companion Julia script. **Open** denotes a question without a claimed result. Equalities use real arithmetic. The operations used below appear in the [embedding](../../src/embedding.jl), [attention](../../src/attention.jl), [block](../../src/block.jl) and [readout](../../src/model.jl) implementations.

## 1. Worlds, predictors and errors

**Definition (world tower).** At level i take a finite token alphabet and a probability law on paths, equivalently a compatible family of finite-prefix laws. Work at specified prediction times. A refinement has a history map π, an outcome map ρ and a coarse history update u satisfying π(hx) = u(πh,ρx). Coordinatewise token merging uses the same alphabet map for both. For a context window π takes a suffix and ρ is the identity, with u dropping the oldest token when full. Here the observed token path law stays the same; the compression is in the information supplied to the predictor. For time blocking, sample at block boundaries, take an entire next fine block as the outcome, then apply ρ. Its law requires the joint autoregressive block prediction. A choice of initial law is part of the world. World consistency means the coarse path law equals the image of the fine law at the aligned times. Maps and pushforwards compose from finer to coarser levels.

**Definition (predictive quotient).** Give each level a model kernel Mᵢ(next outcome | history), for PicoGPT the softmax of the last logit column. A lift is a parameter map from parent to child. Its requirement at birth, on a stated set of fine histories h, is

$$\rho_* M_2(\cdot\mid h)=M_1(\cdot\mid\pi h). \tag{1}$$

Pointwise on the model input domain and almost surely under the world are different specifications. World accuracy is a separate comparison of each Mᵢ with the corresponding world conditional. Apply (1) to the world kernels themselves to define predictive sufficiency. Mere consistency of path laws gives a conditional *mixture* of the left side over fine histories given πh. Equality before mixing is stronger. A lift can satisfy (1) at birth even if the fine world violates predictive sufficiency. Subsequent learning can then improve fine predictions by breaking (1).

**Definition (controlled version).** Specify a controlled kernel for the joint next observation and reward given history and action, a surjection α on admissible actions at each history, and a common decision clock. Require (1) for that joint kernel at every admissible (h,a), with coarse action α(a), consistent initial laws and coarse rewards in common units. Require the available coarse actions to depend only on πh. Histories update using the coarse action, observation and observed reward. On a blocked clock include the accumulated discounted reward of the segment and the continuation discount (and termination) in the outcome when these vary.

**Proved (returns).** Any coarse policy lifts by choosing a representative or a distribution of representatives of each selected action. Induction on the horizon with the joint kernel gives the same coarse trajectory law and hence the same law of the discounted return. Backward induction gives equality of optimal expected finite-horizon values even allowing the fine controller to use its history, because each fine action has the value of its coarse class and every class is attainable. Bounded rewards and discount strictly below one permit passage to the infinite horizon by a uniform tail bound. Equality of expected return for one policy alone imposes none of the other action-conditional equalities (take identically zero rewards and arbitrary observations).

**Definition (internal quotient).** Write the embedding as e, the successive stream updates as Fʲ and the probability readout, including final LayerNorm, as g. Align stages by grouping blocks or inserting identity stages. At aligned boundaries take maps Rⱼ from fine to coarse streams, with
$$R_0 e_2=e_1\pi,\qquad R_j F_2^j=F_1^j R_{j-1},\qquad \rho_*g_2=g_1 R_L. \tag{2}$$
State the domain at each boundary. A quotient on all streams is stronger than one on reachable streams. A section J with RJ = I and F₂J = JF₁ only proves the block identity on im J.

**Proved (existence of a quotient block).** For fixed Rⱼ₋₁,Rⱼ an induced update on the image of Rⱼ₋₁ exists iff any two streams with equal Rⱼ₋₁-images also have equal Rⱼ-images after F₂ʲ. Necessity follows from (2). For sufficiency, define the induced update at a coarse stream by choosing any preimage; the condition makes its value unique. To obtain the specified parent block one must also identify this induced map with F₁ʲ on that image. Generic trained child weights need not pass the fibre test.

**Proved (implications).** Composing (2) cancels intermediate maps and proves (1). Block intertwining alone leaves the readout free. Conversely take two same-width, one-block models with constant identical logits, two distinct token columns in the parent, and coincident token columns in the child (i.e. copy only the positional code, set child E = 0). Their predictions agree at every history but no single-valued R₀ can satisfy the embedding condition at the first position. Even with identical distinct embeddings one can prescribe R = I, use different residual biases, and keep constant logits. Output equality identifies neither layer dynamics nor coordinates.

**Proved (errors across levels).** For each edge at its matched history put eᵢ = TV(ρ₍ᵢ₎* Mᵢ₊₁,Mᵢ) and kᵢ = KL(ρ₍ᵢ₎* Mᵢ₊₁ ∥ Mᵢ), in nats, with TV half the ℓ¹ distance. The end-to-end TV is at most min(1, Σeᵢ), by contraction of TV under a map and the triangle inequality. Each eᵢ ≤ √(kᵢ/2) by Pinsker. The same bounds hold in expectation using the compatible history marginals, with Jensen for the square root, or uniformly with suprema. KL measures excess expected log loss under the first law. It contracts under pushforward by the log-sum inequality but has no triangle inequality. For identity maps, Bernoulli parameters 0.8, 0.5, 0.2 already violate the putative additive KL bound. On a finite alphabet, if every endpoint coarse mass is ≥ m > 0, KL(P ∥ Q) ≤ Σ(P−Q)²/Q ≤ 4 TV(P,Q)²/m, using log u ≤ u−1. Without a support condition TV alone gives no finite KL bound.

**Proved (errors across time).** With matched initial histories, the path KL over n steps *does* add the expected conditional KLs, by factoring path probabilities and summing their log ratios under the first path law. When a coarse predictor uses a compressed history, define its generated path law by feeding it that statistic after each step. For a projected fine path the next conditional given the entire observed coarse past is a mixture over hidden histories, so convexity bounds each term by the expected fine-history edge KL. Initial relative entropy adds if the initial laws differ. Uniform conditional TV ≤ δ gives path TV ≤ 1−(1−δ)ⁿ by maximal coupling while paths agree. For a bounded return with range length C on this horizon, the resulting expected-return discrepancy is ≤ C times path TV. These statements also apply under matched policies.

**Proved (approximate internal quotient).** In chosen stream norms bound each commutator defect in (2) by ηⱼ on the compared trajectories, including embedding defect η₀. For Kⱼ-Lipschitz parent updates the stream discrepancy satisfies dⱼ ≤ ηⱼ + Kⱼ dⱼ₋₁ by adding and subtracting F₁ʲ Rⱼ₋₁. Thus dᴸ ≤ Σⱼ₌₀ᴸ ηⱼ ∏ₖ₌ⱼ₊₁ᴸ Kₖ. A readout with TV Lipschitz constant K and terminal TV defect η gives (1) to η + K dᴸ. Constants need only hold on the sets used in the comparison.

## 2. Birth maps inside the network

**Conventions.** The parent weights can be arbitrary. Operators are columnwise except for attention. An “old” tangent varies the parent through the chosen lift. A “new” tangent is an additional independent variation. A function-preserving lift constrains the value at birth, with no general constraint forcing old gradients to vanish. Gradients below refer to the data loss with the Euclidean parameter metric, before an optimizer step. Write G for the downstream stream covector, and ⟨·,·⟩ for the Frobenius pairing, with sums over all columns. Symmetries below are the copy or label permutations relevant to each construction. Full parameter stabilizers can be larger for special parents (zero matrices are an extreme example). At differentiable points an invariant loss at a fixed point of an orthogonal permutation group has invariant gradient, by differentiating the invariance. Every tangent with zero group average then has zero directional derivative. Data and fixed positional codes must also respect the group. In particular, preserving the forward function does not restrict optimization to new directions. Freezing inherited parameters or constraining the update would be a separate training rule.

### Vocabulary

**Proved (both ends).** For each coarse v take a nonempty fibre Bᵥ and strictly positive weights qᵥᵦ summing to one. Copy E[:,v] to every child input (v,b). Copy the blocks, positional code and final LayerNorm; copy readout row wᵥ and set the child bias to cᵥ + log qᵥᵦ. Every fine history now produces the parent stream on its merged history (R = I). The exponentiated child logits sum to exp zᵥ within each fibre, so the normalizer and coarse probabilities agree. Conversely the parent input and readout can be recovered on the lift by picking a representative embedding and row, and taking logsumexp of its fibre's biases. A target (v,b) costs −log pᵥ − log qᵥᵦ. Uniform splitting needs the bias correction even when the new rows are identical, unless all fibres have the same size (then it is a common logit shift).

**Proved (scope of conditional splitting).** For any normalized q(b|v,h) the probability factorization and loss identity hold as kernel algebra. An affine readout on the inherited feature f can realize a prescribed split precisely if the desired child log probabilities, up to a single additive scalar function of f shared by all rows, are affine functions of f and are well-defined at each f. This follows by taking log odds against one reference row. In particular the child cannot distinguish histories with the same inherited f using the copied input. Even splitting one row with an unsplit reference row requires log q(b|v,h) affine in f on the attainable set (subtract the two parent logits). Arbitrary conditional q needs extra capacity, e.g. an explicitly normalized conditional head. Zero probabilities require masks or a limit of logits.

**Proved (symmetry and gradients).** Parent variations move all siblings together. Relative embeddings and relative logit rows/biases are new. With uniform q the parameter arrangement is fixed by ∏ᵥ Sym(Bᵥ) relabelling inputs and outputs; for unequal q only equal-weight permutations fix the biases. On a fine labelled example the objective generally breaks this symmetry. For one target y, the fine logit covector is pᵥ qᵥᵦ − 1{y=(v,b)}, by differentiating logsumexp. Its sum in each fibre is the parent covector. Each row gradient is that scalar times fᵀ and each bias gradient that scalar. At the input each child's column collects the parent stream adjoints only from occurrences of that fine token, since the copied readout rows sum the outgoing covectors back to the parent ones. Thus embedding gradients sum to the parent gradient and sibling contrasts can learn on the first step. For a coarse-labelled loss at birth, the fine logit covectors are qᵥᵦ times their parent covectors. A symmetric *full objective* is needed to infer zero gradients on all antisymmetric parameter directions, including input embeddings.

### Depth and repeated passes

**Proved (gated pass).** Write Bφ for one pre-LayerNorm block. Insert X ↦ X + a(Bφ(X)−X), a = 0. It is the identity on the entire stream space. Erasing the insertion gives the parent; aligned stages use R = I. The gate derivative is ⟨G,Bφ(X)−X⟩, which can have either sign or vanish. The insertion contributes zero to every φ gradient at birth. If φ is shared with earlier passes it still receives their gradients. A per-pass gate is required for this argument with a nonidentity shared block. Simply repeating that block can change X (choose a nonzero output bias and zero residual weight matrices). Executing the old passes in order is the inherited computation. The extra gate and any untied block weights are new. A closed gate makes its entire branch unobservable; this degeneracy requires no permutation symmetry, and the ordered passes have no general exchange symmetry.

**Proved (stock untied block).** The source has no gates. For an extra ordinary block set Wo, bo, W2, b2 to zero, with other weights free. Both residual increments vanish. Their output matrix gradients are G Oᵀ and G U₊ᵀ, with U₊ the ReLU activations. Both output biases receive G summed over columns, while incoming branch weights and LayerNorms receive zero. This follows by differentiating each affine output, since the derivative in X of each closed increment is zero. This also explains the different trainability of a scalar gate and zero output matrices.

### Heads and MLP hidden units

**Proved (head addition with fixed old head shapes).** In an attention module allowing extra projections of unchanged head dimension, concatenate a new head output Oₙ, append zero columns C to Wo and keep bo. The stream update is unchanged for every X. Erase the appended rows and columns to recover the parent. The same affine argument gives ∇C = G Oₙᵀ and zero gradients for the new Q,K,V parameters. The covector G here is taken just after the attention residual addition. Old active heads retain their gradients. Heads, with their matching projections and output columns, admit label permutations. Identical unused heads have a permutation stabilizer; distinct unused initializations are all invisible at birth but generally have trivial permutation stabilizer. Only the appended parameters are new.

**Proved (obstruction in stock Config).** At fixed D, raising H forces d = D/H to shrink. One cannot in general keep the original head and mute the new one. Take D = 4, H = 1, one position, unit LayerNorm gain, zero offsets, Wv = Wo = I. The attention increment is LN(x) and its range spans the three-dimensional mean-zero space, even affinely. With H′ = 2 and one muted head, every increment lies in one affine subspace of dimension at most two, including any output bias. Choose four centered input columns with affinely independent normalized columns (e.g. the centered coordinate basis); the free embedding realizes them at position one. This refutes the proposed head-by-head lift. It does not preclude alternative reimplementations using both heads or other layers. Keeping d fixed while increasing D also incurs the width and position conditions below. At fixed D there is also a constructive sufficient condition. Adjoin a bias coordinate to each normalized input and write Uq, Uk, Uv for a head’s augmented affine matrices and O for its columns in Wo. If UqᵀUk and O Uv each have rank at most d′, factor them as AᵀB and C V with inner dimension d′. Choose Uq′ = (d′/d)¹ᐟ⁴ A, Uk′ = (d′/d)¹ᐟ⁴ B, Uv′ = V, O′ = C. Scores after division by √d′ and projected values now coincide. Put one such factorization in an active slice per old head, set extra output columns to zero and keep bo. This proves a sufficient rank test, allowing for further degeneracies such as query-dependent row shifts.

**Proved (MLP width).** Append rows U and bias c to W1,b1 and zero columns C to W2, keeping b2. The new increment is C relu(U LN₂(X)+c). Erasing those units recovers the old update on all streams. ∇C = G relu(U LN₂(X)+c)ᵀ, with zero incoming gradients, using the code's zero pullback at a ReLU kink. A unit with both its activation and its outgoing weight zero has no first-order signal on that input. Relabelling hidden units and matching output columns gives the permutation group of the hidden layer. Identically initialized extra units retain their permutations and their gradient differences vanish. Differently initialized extra rows can receive different outgoing gradients. Old units vary as before.

### Residual width

**Proved (layerwise section).** Let D′ = rD, r a positive integer, and keep H fixed, so d′ = rd. Within each head repeat each old coordinate r times, in any fixed ordering that keeps each head contiguous. Let J denote this matrix and R = Jᵀ/r. Then RJ = I, JᵀJ = rI, and JR is the orthogonal projection onto equal copies. Set γ′ = Jγ, β′ = Jβ in *all* LayerNorms. The mean and mean square of Jx equal those of x exactly, hence LN′(Jx) = J LN(x) with the same ε. Componentwise ReLU likewise commutes with repetition.

| Parameter | Lift |
| --- | --- |
| E | JE, provided the positions also lift |
| Wq,bq and Wk,bk | c JW R, c Jb for each, c = r⁻¹ᐟ⁴ |
| Wv,bv and Wo,bo | JW R, Jb for each |
| W1,b1 and W2,b2 | W1 R, b1 and JW2, Jb2 (keep F fixed) |
| Wout,bout | Wout R, bout |

**Proved (constants and inverse on the section).** The new score in each head is c²r qᵀk/√(rd) = qᵀk/√d. More generally the two multipliers need product r⁻¹ᐟ². Attention probabilities coincide, so the mixed values are JO. Both residual additions respect J. The MLP can also repeat each of its F units s times using Jꜰ and Rꜰ = Jꜰᵀ/s, giving W1′ = Jꜰ W1 R, b1′ = Jꜰ b1, W2′ = JW2 Rꜰ. ReLU gives the same result; s = r maintains F′ = 4D′ when F = 4D. The final affine readout contracts with R and recovers exactly the old logits. To recover an old weight, contract its repeated output index with its averaging map and expand its repeated input index with J (or Jꜰ), undoing c where used. This proves F₂J = JF₁. It gives (2) along lifted trajectories if the embedding enters this section.

**Proved (failure off the section).** Write x′ = Jx + n, Rn = 0. Its mean is the old mean, but its variance increases by ‖n‖²/(rD), by orthogonality. Even with unit gains, averaging the normalized column gives the old centered numerator divided by this larger standard deviation. For a nonconstant x and nonzero n, R LN′(x′) ≠ LN(Rx′). Set one position, Wv = Wo = I, unit gains and zero MLP increment to transfer this discrepancy directly to R F₂ versus F₁ R. The average is generally a left inverse on the image, not a global block quotient.

**Proved (positional obstruction in the source).** In positions(D,T), sine/cosine pair i has frequency 10000^(−2(i−1)/D). With D′ = rD the old frequencies survive at pair indices 1+r(i−1), but extra pairs have intermediate frequencies. No permutation of duplicates of the old code can create them in general. Already D = 2, D′ = 4, one head, T = 2 gives phases (1, 0.01) instead of (1, 1) in the second column. Matching the first column pairs like trigonometric components; their increments to the second column then differ, so even an arbitrary position-independent offset of each embedding cannot fix the section. For an output witness use E = 0, L = 0, unit final gains, zero biases and two logit rows (1,0) and (0,0). Use Jx = (x₁,x₂,x₁,x₂) and the table's readout. At position two the old first logit is positive since sin(1) > cos(1). The new first logit is negative since sin(1)+sin(0.01) < cos(1)+cos(0.01). Even the predicted probabilities change. This refutes coordinate duplication as an unrestricted lift for stock PicoGPT. A supplied P′ = JP fixes it, and it works in stock code on a single position with appropriately ordered copies, or on other inputs satisfying the entry condition. A universal lift by some different reparameterization is open here.

**Proved (width symmetry and gradients).** With a copied code, permute the r copies independently for each original stream coordinate, acting on every incident parameter index. This gives a stabilizer containing (Sᵣ)ᴰ, with analogous permutations for duplicated hidden units. In stream space im J is old and ker R is the space of copy differences. In parameter space the old tangent is the image of the derivative of the table's lift Λ. The gradient has zero contraction with all variations of zero group average. Inherited derivatives obey (DΛ)ᵀ ∇ℓ₂ = ∇ℓ₁ by the chain rule. This identity concerns the pullback, and need not yield equal Euclidean step sizes in both models. In this particular table one can also see the invisible matrix directions directly: forward activations in each duplicated space are equal copies and backward covectors are equal copies, since each operation and its pullback commute with permutations there. Affine matrix gradients are outer products of those arrays, summed over columns. Thus contrasts between individual entries of one replicated tile have zero derivative, including invariant contrasts between its diagonal and off-diagonal entries. Generic stock P′ breaks the copy symmetry; these zero-gradient conclusions then have no general force.

### Context length

**Proved (prefix).** At fixed D all frequencies are unchanged, so positions(D,T′)[:,1:T] = positions(D,T) for T′ ≥ T. Change the context cap, keep θ identical. At any input length ≤ T the programs coincide. Even on a longer sequence, restricting every stream to its first T columns commutes with every block (one sequence at a time). Each retained row of causal attention reads only retained columns, and the other operations are tokenwise. Thus prefix restriction is a global block quotient at those columns. For a loss on the retained predictions, later columns have zero adjoints, and all parameter derivatives are exactly the parent's. The cap adds no parameters. Histories past the cap are the new input domain. The causal order and fixed phases distinguish time indices; no nonidentity position permutation preserves that order.

**Proved (suffix).** Comparing *the last prediction* on a long history to a parent run on its last T tokens changes the question. Stock generation re-embeds the cropped window at indices starting from one. With D = 2 and the preceding zero-block parent, a repeated token at native index two gives a positive first logit; the same token at cropped index one gives a negative one. This failure uses no attention at all. Exact suffix agreement can be imposed by cropping and reindexing before embedding (or isolating that suffix in every layer and giving it the parent's phases). Attention masks alone cannot reset the positional phases. A moving local causal band also lets earlier tokens enter retained states indirectly via multiple layers. Hard-cropped positions carry zero signal for the suffix loss; learning to use longer histories under the unmodified larger cap uses already existing parameters. No new gate occurs in the source.

### Blocked positions

**Proved (one attention row).** At a block boundary and for each fully visible source block B, put sⱼ = qᵀkⱼ/√d. The sum of its attention weights is exactly the coarse softmax with block logit ℓᴮ(q) = log Σⱼ∈ᴮ exp(sⱼ). The value representing B in the mixed output is vᴮ(q) = Σⱼ∈ᴮ softmax(s|B)ⱼ vⱼ. Both formulas follow by partitioning the fine denominator and numerator. Only differences of block logits matter. For two equally sized blocks of scalar keys (−1,1) and (0,0), d = 1, their log-odds is log cosh(q), with strictly positive second derivative at zero. A pair of fixed effective keys gives an affine log-odds in q, even allowing block bias. Taking constant values one and zero in the respective blocks makes the block weight observable. This obstruction applies even on three query values −a,0,a. Such scores are admissible in a stock head: use D even ≥ 4, H = D, and independent linear key and query rows on the mean-zero LayerNorm feature space. Distinct tokens at fixed positions can realize prescribed sufficiently small features in that space by adjusting E (ε > 0). Keep the two source blocks fixed, vary the query at a later token and compare the two blocks' odds; other visible keys cancel. The claim concerns closure of one ordinary attention head with the inherited query, not all possible predictors on a finite corpus.

**Proved (exception, symmetries and birth).** If every visible key in a block is identical, its logit is qᵀkᴮ/√d + log |B| and its value is the arithmetic mean. Equal visible block sizes cancel the size term. Restrict to queries with whole blocks visible or count the visible subset explicitly. In this restricted row, replicating a key/value pair into the same number of identical copies in every block is a section, and aggregation recovers that row. Paired permutations of (key,value) entries within a visible block leave its sums invariant. At the replicated point the full product of within-block permutation groups fixes the arrays; a row loss gives equal per-copy adjoints, each the coarse derivative divided by the multiplicity, by permutation invariance and the chain rule. Mean variations are old, zero-sum contrasts have zero first derivative. Unequal values, partial causal masks and the positional code can break this stabilizer for the full computation. For generic blocking there is no such network lift, hence no birth parameter gradient or canonical split into old and new. A causal block's internal fine predictions also require a within-block decoder if the target includes them.

## 3. When does a world close?

**Proved (three existence questions).** Every path map defines a coarse law by taking its pushforward. A coarse kernel that agrees with the prediction from *each fine history* exists precisely when the pushed conditional is constant on every relevant history fibre, by the same representative argument as for a quotient block. Realizing that kernel in PicoGPT further requires it to belong to the chosen model family; for example, finite softmax logits cannot realize a deterministic binary transition exactly.

**Proved (Markov criterion).** Let K(x,y) be a row-stochastic matrix, π a state partition, and C(x,A) = 1{πx = A}. There is one coarse transition matrix Q giving the correct next coarse state from *every* fine state iff KC = CQ. Entrywise, Σᵧ∈ᴮ K(x,y) must be constant for x in a given block A, for each target block B. Necessity follows by starting at x; sufficiency follows by defining Q(A,B) to be that common row sum. Inductively KⁿC = CQⁿ, and successive conditioning gives all coarse path probabilities. This is strong lumpability. A projected chain can be Markov for a particular initial law without satisfying this criterion. For instance let fine states x,y,z have transitions x→z, y→y, z→z and merge x,y. Starting at y the coarse state is constant, but the probabilities of reaching {z} from x,y differ. For control apply the row-sum test to the joint reward/next-class outcome for every pair of representatives of the same state/action class. These are world conditions independent of whether the coarse kernel fits in a specified neural model. [Buchholz, *Exact and ordinary lumpability in finite Markov chains*](https://doi.org/10.2307/3215235)

**Proved (predictive statistic and its cost).** Let H be a random fine history, S = πH and Y a future coarse outcome. The best unrestricted coarse predictor is Q*(·|S) = Law(Y|S), the conditional mixture over the fibre. For any coarse predictor Q the expected discrepancy decomposes as
$$\mathbb E\,D_{\rm KL}(P(Y\mid H)\Vert Q(Y\mid S))
 = I(Y;H\mid S)+\mathbb E\,D_{\rm KL}(Q^*(Y\mid S)\Vert Q(Y\mid S)).$$
Insert Q* into the log ratio and condition its second term on S. Hence the irreducible cost of forgetting is the conditional mutual information, zero iff Y is conditionally independent of H given S, almost surely. Exactness for every allowed history needs the stronger pointwise statement on each fibre, including any specified off-support kernels. A restricted coarse model may have additional approximation error even at zero information loss.

**Proved (history-dependent worlds).** For a recursively updated statistic S as in §1, apply the one-step conditional independence test at every time and every relevant history. Induction (integrating out fine next symbols with the same ρ-image) then factors the law of every future coarse cylinder through S. Conversely equality of future laws on a fibre gives the one-step test by marginalization. Without a specified recursively closed update one must check the whole future or also establish update closure. For example a fine history ending in two bits 01 or 11 has the same last bit, but under the rule “next bit equals the penultimate bit” the one-bit truncation loses the prediction. In general two histories are predictively equivalent if their conditional laws for all finite coarse futures agree. Their equivalence class is the coarsest statistic sufficient for those futures: any sufficient statistic can only identify histories inside a class. For fully observed histories of the coarse process this is the predictive (causal-state) construction. For a projection with hidden fine history, conditioning *only* on the observed coarse history gives a posterior mixture and may require arbitrarily long memory; recursive update on coarse observations alone must still be justified for a statistic defined on fine histories. For control, require equality under all future coarse interventions and include the reward in the future. [Shalizi and Crutchfield, *Computational Mechanics: Pattern and Prediction, Structure and Simplicity*](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)

**Proved (a finite fine chain with unbounded coarse memory).** Let x,y both emit A, z emit B. In x or y, stay with probability a or b respectively and otherwise go to z, with 0 < b < a < 1. From z stay with probability 1/2 or enter x,y with probability 1/4 each. After B followed by n ≥ 1 observations of A the odds of x against y are (a/b)ⁿ⁻¹, so the probability of another A is (aⁿ+bⁿ)/(aⁿ⁻¹+bⁿ⁻¹). This strictly increases with n. Starting from z, two histories at the same time can end in runs of any two feasible lengths (spend the earlier ticks at z). Arbitrarily long identical suffixes thus admit different predictions even after marginalizing hidden histories. A posterior on x,y suffices, while a fixed suffix length does not.

## 4. Fixed points and relevance

**Definition (one-scale relevance).** Relative to a specified history law, output observable and log loss, a forgotten history distinction has predictive cost I(Y;H|S). For a smooth family of strictly positive finite coarse model outputs pθ(h) = ρ* Mθ(h), fix the history measure μ independently of θ. At a given θ a perturbation v is invisible to first order precisely when Dpθ(h)v = 0 for μ-almost every h. This tests predictions. A decision criterion instead needs a policy class, horizon and return functional, since two different forecasts can recommend the same action.

**Proved (local metric).** For finite history support, Taylor expansion and Σᵧ pθ(y|h) = 1 give the expected KL from pθ to pθ₊ₜᵥ as (t²/2) Eμ Σᵧ ((Dpθ v)ᵧ)²/pθ(y|h) + o(t²). Its null space is exactly the first-order invisible subspace above. Finite changes and higher-order derivatives can reveal directions in that null space (e.g. a gated product ab at a = b = 0). Sensitivity eigenvalues need a metric on perturbations for comparison; a change of parameter units changes an ordinary Euclidean ranking.

**Proved (fixed point requires a scale map).** A projective family Wᵢ = πᵢ* Wᵢ₊₁ consists of points in different spaces. To ask a fixed-point question specify a common space of laws (or models modulo observational equivalence), a repeatable blocking with a clock and field identification, and a well-defined endomorphism B after rescaling. If an approximate projection back into a model family is used, it too is part of B. A fixed point obeys B(W*) = W*. If B is differentiable there, eigenvectors of its derivative with |λ| > 1 or < 1 grow or shrink under iterated linearized coarse-graining, respectively; |λ| = 1 requires further analysis. This convention uses the coarse-graining direction. Parameter symmetries must be removed if exponents are to characterize observables.

**Proved (example).** In the family of iid signs with mean m, replace each disjoint pair by its product and count each pair as one tick. The resulting signs are independent with mean m². Thus m = 0 and m = 1 are fixed. The derivative is 0 at the unbiased fixed point and 2 at the ordered one (one-sided perturbations there). The blocking and identification supply the eigenvalues. They do not follow from the alphabet or the transformer.

**Open.** For the unspecified PicoGPT tower there is no specified endomorphism and consequently no defined RG fixed point or spectrum. Whether a particular world tower admits a predictive quotient of bounded complexity, whether one can learn it, and whether growing costs less at matched accuracy require additional hypotheses or experiments. Exactness of the birth maps supplies none of those bounds.
