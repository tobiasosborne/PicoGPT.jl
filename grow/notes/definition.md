# When does a grown PicoGPT have its parent as a quotient?

**Scope.** Definitions specify the objects. Paragraphs labelled **Proved** include their argument or refer to an earlier proof. **Checked** refers to the finite witnesses in the companion Julia script. **Open** denotes a question without a claimed result. Equalities use real arithmetic.

## 1. Worlds, predictors and errors

**Definition (world tower).** At level i take a finite token alphabet and a probability law on paths, equivalently a compatible family of finite-prefix laws. Work at specified prediction times. A refinement has a history map π, an outcome map ρ and a coarse history update u satisfying π(hx) = u(πh,ρx). Coordinatewise token merging uses the same alphabet map for both. A context window uses a suffix for π and the identity for ρ, with u dropping the oldest token when full. For time blocking, sample at block boundaries, take an entire next fine block as the outcome, then apply ρ. Its law requires the joint autoregressive block prediction. A choice of initial law is part of the world. World consistency means the coarse path law equals the image of the fine law at the aligned times. Maps and pushforwards compose from finer to coarser levels.

**Definition (predictive quotient).** Give each level a model kernel Mᵢ(next outcome | history), for PicoGPT the softmax of the last logit column. A lift is a parameter map from parent to child. Its requirement at birth, on a stated set of fine histories h, is

$$\rho_* M_2(\cdot\mid h)=M_1(\cdot\mid\pi h). \tag{1}$$

Pointwise on the model input domain and almost surely under the world are different specifications. World accuracy is a separate comparison of each Mᵢ with the corresponding world conditional. Apply (1) to the world kernels themselves to define predictive sufficiency. Mere consistency of path laws gives a conditional *mixture* of the left side over fine histories given πh. Equality before mixing is stronger.

**Definition (controlled version).** Specify a controlled kernel for the joint next observation and reward given history and action, a surjection α on admissible actions at each history, and a common decision clock. Require (1) for that joint kernel at every admissible (h,a), with coarse action α(a), consistent initial laws and coarse rewards in common units. Require the available coarse actions to depend only on πh. Histories update using the coarse action, observation and observed reward. On a blocked clock include the accumulated discounted reward of the segment and the continuation discount (and termination) in the outcome when these vary.

**Proved (returns).** Any coarse policy lifts by choosing a representative or a distribution of representatives of each selected action. Induction on the horizon with the joint kernel gives the same coarse trajectory law and hence the same law of the discounted return. Backward induction gives equality of optimal expected finite-horizon values even allowing the fine controller to use its history, because each fine action has the value of its coarse class and every class is attainable. Bounded rewards and discount strictly below one permit passage to the infinite horizon by a uniform tail bound. Equality of expected return for one policy alone imposes none of the other action-conditional equalities (take identically zero rewards and arbitrary observations).

**Definition (internal quotient).** Write the embedding as e, the successive stream updates as Fʲ and the probability readout, including final LayerNorm, as g. Align stages by grouping blocks or inserting identity stages. At aligned boundaries take maps Rⱼ from fine to coarse streams, with
$$R_0 e_2=e_1\pi,\qquad R_j F_2^j=F_1^j R_{j-1},\qquad \rho_*g_2=g_1 R_L. \tag{2}$$
State the domain at each boundary. A quotient on all streams is stronger than one on reachable streams. A section J with RJ = I and F₂J = JF₁ only proves the block identity on im J.

**Proved (implications).** Composing (2) cancels intermediate maps and proves (1). Block intertwining alone leaves the readout free. Conversely take two same-width, one-block models with constant identical logits, two distinct token columns in the parent, and coincident token columns in the child (i.e. copy only the positional code, set child E = 0). Their predictions agree at every history but no single-valued R₀ can satisfy the embedding condition at the first position. Even with identical distinct embeddings one can prescribe R = I, use different residual biases, and keep constant logits. Output equality identifies neither layer dynamics nor coordinates.

**Proved (errors across levels).** For each edge at its matched history put eᵢ = TV(ρ₍ᵢ₎* Mᵢ₊₁,Mᵢ) and kᵢ = KL(ρ₍ᵢ₎* Mᵢ₊₁ ∥ Mᵢ), in nats, with TV half the ℓ¹ distance. The end-to-end TV is at most min(1, Σeᵢ), by contraction of TV under a map and the triangle inequality. Each eᵢ ≤ √(kᵢ/2) by Pinsker. The same bounds hold in expectation using the compatible history marginals, with Jensen for the square root, or uniformly with suprema. KL measures excess expected log loss under the first law. It contracts under pushforward by the log-sum inequality but has no triangle inequality. For identity maps, Bernoulli parameters 0.8, 0.5, 0.2 already violate the putative additive KL bound. On a finite alphabet, if every endpoint coarse mass is ≥ m > 0, KL(P ∥ Q) ≤ Σ(P−Q)²/Q ≤ 4 TV(P,Q)²/m, using log u ≤ u−1. Without a support condition TV alone gives no finite KL bound.

**Proved (errors across time).** With matched initial histories, the path KL over n steps *does* add the expected conditional KLs, by factoring path probabilities and summing their log ratios under the first path law. For a projected fine path its conditional is a mixture over hidden histories, so convexity bounds each term by the expected fine-history edge KL. Initial relative entropy adds if the initial laws differ. Uniform conditional TV ≤ δ gives path TV ≤ 1−(1−δ)ⁿ by maximal coupling while paths agree. For a bounded return with range length C on this horizon, the resulting expected-return discrepancy is ≤ C times path TV. These statements also apply under matched policies.

**Proved (approximate internal quotient).** In chosen stream norms bound each commutator defect in (2) by ηⱼ on the compared trajectories, including embedding defect η₀. For Kⱼ-Lipschitz parent updates the stream discrepancy satisfies dⱼ ≤ ηⱼ + Kⱼ dⱼ₋₁ by adding and subtracting F₁ʲ Rⱼ₋₁. Thus dᴸ ≤ Σⱼ₌₀ᴸ ηⱼ ∏ₖ₌ⱼ₊₁ᴸ Kₖ. A readout with TV Lipschitz constant K and terminal TV defect η gives (1) to η + K dᴸ. Constants need only hold on the sets used in the comparison.
