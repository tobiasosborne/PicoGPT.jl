# When does a grown PicoGPT have its parent as a quotient?

**Scope.** Definitions specify the objects. Paragraphs labelled **Proved** include their argument or refer to an earlier proof. **Checked** refers to the finite witnesses in the companion Julia script. **Open** denotes a question without a claimed result. Equalities use real arithmetic.

## 1. Worlds, predictors and errors

**Definition (world tower).** At level i take a finite token alphabet and a probability law on paths, equivalently a compatible family of finite-prefix laws. Work at specified prediction times. A refinement has a history map π, an outcome map ρ and a coarse history update u satisfying π(hx) = u(πh,ρx). Coordinatewise token merging uses the same alphabet map for both. A context window uses a suffix for π and the identity for ρ, with u dropping the oldest token when full. For time blocking, sample at block boundaries, take an entire next fine block as the outcome, then apply ρ. Its law requires the joint autoregressive block prediction. A choice of initial law is part of the world. World consistency means the coarse path law equals the image of the fine law at the aligned times. Maps and pushforwards compose from finer to coarser levels.

**Definition (predictive quotient).** Give each level a model kernel Mᵢ(next outcome | history), for PicoGPT the softmax of the last logit column. A lift is a parameter map from parent to child. Its requirement at birth, on a stated set of fine histories h, is

$$\rho_* M_2(\cdot\mid h)=M_1(\cdot\mid\pi h). \tag{1}$$

Pointwise on the model input domain and almost surely under the world are different specifications. World accuracy is a separate comparison of each Mᵢ with the corresponding world conditional. Apply (1) to the world kernels themselves to define predictive sufficiency. Mere consistency of path laws gives a conditional *mixture* of the left side over fine histories given πh. Equality before mixing is stronger.

**Definition (controlled version).** Specify a controlled kernel for the joint next observation and reward given history and action, a surjection α on admissible actions at each history, and a common decision clock. Require (1) for that joint kernel at every admissible (h,a), with coarse action α(a), consistent initial laws and coarse rewards in common units. Require the available coarse actions to depend only on πh. Histories update using the coarse action, observation and observed reward. On a blocked clock include the accumulated discounted reward of the segment and the continuation discount (and termination) in the outcome when these vary.

**Proved (returns).** Any coarse policy lifts by choosing a representative or a distribution of representatives of each selected action. Induction on the horizon with the joint kernel gives the same coarse trajectory law and hence the same law of the discounted return. Backward induction gives equality of optimal expected finite-horizon values even allowing the fine controller to use its history, because each fine action has the value of its coarse class and every class is attainable. Bounded rewards and discount strictly below one permit passage to the infinite horizon by a uniform tail bound. Equality of expected return for one policy alone imposes none of the other action-conditional equalities (take identically zero rewards and arbitrary observations).

**Definition (internal quotient).** Write the embedding as e, the successive stream updates as Fʲ and the probability readout, including final LayerNorm, as g. Align stages by grouping blocks or inserting identity stages. At aligned boundaries take maps Rⱼ from fine to coarse streams, with
$$R_0 e_2=e_1\pi,\qquad R_j F_2^j=F_1^j R_{j-1},\qquad \rho_*g_2=g_1 R_L. \tag{2}$$
State the domain at each boundary. A quotient on all streams is stronger than one on reachable streams. A section J with RJ = I and F₂J = JF₁ only proves the block identity on im J.

**Proved (implications).** Composing (2) cancels intermediate maps and proves (1). Block intertwining alone leaves the readout free. Conversely take two same-width, one-block models with constant identical logits, two distinct token columns in the parent, and coincident token columns in the child (i.e. copy only the positional code, set child E = 0). Their predictions agree at every history but no single-valued R₀ can satisfy the embedding condition at the first position. Even with identical distinct embeddings one can prescribe R = I, use different residual biases, and keep constant logits. Output equality identifies neither layer dynamics nor coordinates.

**Proved (errors across levels).** For each edge at its matched history put eᵢ = TV(ρ₍ᵢ₎* Mᵢ₊₁,Mᵢ) and kᵢ = KL(ρ₍ᵢ₎* Mᵢ₊₁ ∥ Mᵢ), in nats, with TV half the ℓ¹ distance. The end-to-end TV is at most min(1, Σeᵢ), by contraction of TV under a map and the triangle inequality. Each eᵢ ≤ √(kᵢ/2) by Pinsker. The same bounds hold in expectation using the compatible history marginals, with Jensen for the square root, or uniformly with suprema. KL measures excess expected log loss under the first law. It contracts under pushforward by the log-sum inequality but has no triangle inequality. For identity maps, Bernoulli parameters 0.8, 0.5, 0.2 already violate the putative additive KL bound. On a finite alphabet, if every endpoint coarse mass is ≥ m > 0, KL(P ∥ Q) ≤ Σ(P−Q)²/Q ≤ 4 TV(P,Q)²/m, using log u ≤ u−1. Without a support condition TV alone gives no finite KL bound.

**Proved (errors across time).** With matched initial histories, the path KL over n steps *does* add the expected conditional KLs, by factoring path probabilities and summing their log ratios under the first path law. For a projected fine path its conditional is a mixture over hidden histories, so convexity bounds each term by the expected fine-history edge KL. Initial relative entropy adds if the initial laws differ. Uniform conditional TV ≤ δ gives path TV ≤ 1−(1−δ)ⁿ by maximal coupling while paths agree. For a bounded return with range length C on this horizon, the resulting expected-return discrepancy is ≤ C times path TV. These statements also apply under matched policies.

**Proved (approximate internal quotient).** In chosen stream norms bound each commutator defect in (2) by ηⱼ on the compared trajectories, including embedding defect η₀. For Kⱼ-Lipschitz parent updates the stream discrepancy satisfies dⱼ ≤ ηⱼ + Kⱼ dⱼ₋₁ by adding and subtracting F₁ʲ Rⱼ₋₁. Thus dᴸ ≤ Σⱼ₌₀ᴸ ηⱼ ∏ₖ₌ⱼ₊₁ᴸ Kₖ. A readout with TV Lipschitz constant K and terminal TV defect η gives (1) to η + K dᴸ. Constants need only hold on the sets used in the comparison.

## 2. Birth maps inside the network

**Conventions.** The parent weights can be arbitrary. Operators are columnwise except for attention. An “old” tangent varies the parent through the chosen lift. A “new” tangent is an additional independent variation. A function-preserving lift constrains the value at birth, with no general constraint forcing old gradients to vanish. Gradients below refer to the data loss with the Euclidean parameter metric, before an optimizer step. Write G for the downstream stream covector, and ⟨·,·⟩ for the Frobenius pairing, with sums over all columns. Symmetries below are the copy or label permutations relevant to each construction. Full parameter stabilizers can be larger for special parents (zero matrices are an extreme example). At differentiable points an invariant loss at a fixed point of an orthogonal permutation group has invariant gradient, by differentiating the invariance. Every tangent with zero group average then has zero directional derivative. Data and fixed positional codes must also respect the group.

### Vocabulary

**Proved (both ends).** For each coarse v take a nonempty fibre Bᵥ and strictly positive weights qᵥᵦ summing to one. Copy E[:,v] to every child input (v,b). Copy the blocks, positional code and final LayerNorm; copy readout row wᵥ and set the child bias to cᵥ + log qᵥᵦ. Every fine history now produces the parent stream on its merged history (R = I). The exponentiated child logits sum to exp zᵥ within each fibre, so the normalizer and coarse probabilities agree. Conversely the parent input and readout can be recovered on the lift by picking a representative embedding and row, and taking logsumexp of its fibre's biases. A target (v,b) costs −log pᵥ − log qᵥᵦ. Uniform splitting needs the bias correction even when the new rows are identical, unless all fibres have the same size (then it is a common logit shift).

**Proved (scope of conditional splitting).** For any normalized q(b|v,h) the probability factorization and loss identity hold as kernel algebra. An affine readout on the inherited feature f can realize a prescribed split precisely if the desired child log probabilities, up to a single additive scalar function of f shared by all rows, are affine functions of f and are well-defined at each f. This follows by taking log odds against one reference row. In particular the child cannot distinguish histories with the same inherited f using the copied input. Even splitting one row with an unsplit reference row requires log q(b|v,h) affine in f on the attainable set (subtract the two parent logits). Arbitrary conditional q needs extra capacity, e.g. an explicitly normalized conditional head. Zero probabilities require masks or a limit of logits.

**Proved (symmetry and gradients).** Parent variations move all siblings together. Relative embeddings and relative logit rows/biases are new. With uniform q the parameter arrangement is fixed by ∏ᵥ Sym(Bᵥ) relabelling inputs and outputs; for unequal q only equal-weight permutations fix the biases. On a fine labelled example the objective generally breaks this symmetry. For one target y, the fine logit covector is pᵥ qᵥᵦ − 1{y=(v,b)}, by differentiating logsumexp. Its sum in each fibre is the parent covector. Each row gradient is that scalar times fᵀ and each bias gradient that scalar. At the input each child's column collects the parent stream adjoints only from occurrences of that fine token, since the copied readout rows sum the outgoing covectors back to the parent ones. Thus embedding gradients sum to the parent gradient and sibling contrasts can learn on the first step. For a coarse-labelled loss at birth, the fine logit covectors are qᵥᵦ times their parent covectors. A symmetric *full objective* is needed to infer zero gradients on all antisymmetric parameter directions, including input embeddings.

### Depth and repeated passes

**Proved (gated pass).** Write Bφ for one pre-LayerNorm block. Insert X ↦ X + a(Bφ(X)−X), a = 0. It is the identity on the entire stream space. Erasing the insertion gives the parent; aligned stages use R = I. The gate derivative is ⟨G,Bφ(X)−X⟩, which can have either sign or vanish. The insertion contributes zero to every φ gradient at birth. If φ is shared with earlier passes it still receives their gradients. A per-pass gate is required for this argument with a nonidentity shared block. Simply repeating that block can change X (choose a nonzero output bias and zero residual weight matrices). Executing the old passes in order is the inherited computation. The extra gate and any untied block weights are new. A closed gate makes its entire branch unobservable; this degeneracy requires no permutation symmetry, and the ordered passes have no general exchange symmetry.

**Proved (stock untied block).** The source has no gates. For an extra ordinary block set Wo, bo, W2, b2 to zero, with other weights free. Both residual increments vanish. Their output matrix gradients are G Oᵀ and G U₊ᵀ, with U₊ the ReLU activations. Both output biases receive G summed over columns, while incoming branch weights and LayerNorms receive zero. This follows by differentiating each affine output, since the derivative in X of each closed increment is zero. This also explains the different trainability of a scalar gate and zero output matrices.

### Heads and MLP hidden units

**Proved (head addition with fixed old head shapes).** In an attention module allowing extra projections of unchanged head dimension, concatenate a new head output Oₙ, append zero columns C to Wo and keep bo. The stream update is unchanged for every X. Erase the appended rows and columns to recover the parent. The same affine argument gives ∇C = G Oₙᵀ and zero gradients for the new Q,K,V parameters. The covector G here is taken just after the attention residual addition. Old active heads retain their gradients. Heads, with their matching projections and output columns, admit label permutations. Identical unused heads have a permutation stabilizer; distinct unused initializations are all invisible at birth but generally have trivial permutation stabilizer. Only the appended parameters are new.

**Proved (obstruction in stock Config).** At fixed D, raising H forces d = D/H to shrink. One cannot in general keep the original head and mute the new one. Take D = 4, H = 1, one position, unit LayerNorm gain, zero offsets, Wv = Wo = I. The attention increment is LN(x) and its range spans the three-dimensional mean-zero space, even affinely. With H′ = 2 and one muted head, every increment lies in one affine subspace of dimension at most two, including any output bias. Choose four centered input columns with affinely independent normalized columns (e.g. the centered coordinate basis); the free embedding realizes them at position one. This refutes the proposed head-by-head lift. It does not preclude alternative reimplementations using both heads or other layers. Keeping d fixed while increasing D also incurs the width and position conditions below. At fixed D specially rank-deficient heads can fit the narrower slices if scores and values factor through them, with the score normalization adjusted.

**Proved (MLP width).** Append rows U and bias c to W1,b1 and zero columns C to W2, keeping b2. The new increment is C relu(U LN₂(X)+c). Erasing those units recovers the old update on all streams. ∇C = G relu(U LN₂(X)+c)ᵀ, with zero incoming gradients, using the code's zero pullback at a ReLU kink. A unit with both its activation and its outgoing weight zero has no first-order signal on that input. Relabelling hidden units and matching output columns gives the permutation group of the hidden layer. Identically initialized extra units retain their permutations and their gradient differences vanish. Differently initialized extra rows can receive different outgoing gradients. Old units vary as before.
