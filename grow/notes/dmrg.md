# When does a transformer have a DMRG truncation rule?

**Proved, scope.** Fix a trained predictor, a law of histories and a location in its forward pass. The closest counterpart of White's discarded weight is the quadratic change in the predictor's distribution when a linear bottleneck is inserted there. A single eigendecomposition solves this problem under a separability condition. For the actual attention model the general answer is a weighted subspace fit. Neither answer by itself constructs a narrower PicoGPT. Below, **proved** includes definitions followed by their consequences, **checked numerically** refers to the accompanying Julia script, and **conjectured** labels extrapolations. Width is $D$, head width is $d=D/H$, and $r$ is the retained dimension.

## 1. What replaces the reduced density matrix?

**Proved (reference problem).** Write the normalized superblock wavefunction as a coefficient matrix $\Psi$ in orthonormal left and right bases. For a rank-$r$ orthogonal block projector $Q$, the squared error is $\|(I-Q)\Psi\|_F^2=1-\operatorname{tr}(Q\Psi\Psi^\dagger)$. Maximizing the retained trace keeps the top eigenspace of $\rho=\Psi\Psi^\dagger$. Indeed the diagonal entries of $Q$ in the eigenbasis lie in $[0,1]$ and sum to $r$. Ties across the cut make the minimizer nonunique. The discarded eigenvalue sum is the squared error before renormalizing the state. [White, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.69.2863)

**Proved (single-column intervention).** Take one column $x$ of the residual stream. Put $u=x-m$ and replace $x$ by $x-t(I-Q)u$. At $t=1$ the affine bottleneck encodes $r$ coordinates and decodes back into $D$; $Q^2=Q$, $\operatorname{rank}Q=r$. Set $m=0$ for a linear map, or fix $m=\mathbb E x$ to preserve the ordinary mean. Write $C=\mathbb E uu^\top$ (a covariance only in the latter case). Other inputs to the suffix stay at their observed values. For a scored output with logits $z$, put
$$
p=\operatorname{softmax}(z),\quad S(p)=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top,\quad
J=\partial z/\partial x,\quad G=J^\top S(p)J .
$$
Take the mean of these pullbacks if several outputs are scored. $S$ is the covariance of a categorical one-hot draw, hence $G$ is positive semidefinite. With $\delta z=z_t-z$, teacher-to-student KL is exactly $\log\sum_a p_a e^{\delta z_a}-p^\top\delta z$. Its first derivative at zero vanishes and its Hessian there is $S$. Substituting $\delta z=-tJ(I-Q)u+o(t)$ gives expected KL $=t^2\mathcal E(Q)/2+o(t^2)$, where
$$
\mathcal E(Q)=\mathbb E[u^\top (I-Q)^\top G(I-Q)u].
$$
Assume differentiability on the data and a finite empirical distribution, or sufficient domination to average the remainder. The KL expansion also gives the expected supervised excess log loss if the teacher equals the true conditional law. Otherwise supervised loss generally has a linear term $(p-p_{\rm true})^\top\delta z$ and, for nonlinear logits, an additional second-derivative term. Stationarity in the trained parameters need not cancel a new stream intervention.

**Proved (general projector).** In column-major vectorization the objective is a quadratic form in $\operatorname{vec}(I-Q)$ with coefficient $\mathcal K=\mathbb E[uu^\top\otimes G]$. This fourth-order moment specifies the optimum. An explicit variational construction is to take an orthonormal frame $B$ for a candidate retained plane and $V_\perp$ for its complement. Every projector onto that plane has the form $Q=BB^\top+BK V_\perp^\top$. Set $s=V_\perp^\top u$. For this plane solve the linear normal equations
$$
\mathbb E[B^\top G B\,Kss^\top]=\mathbb E[B^\top G V_\perp\,ss^\top].
$$
They follow by least squares on $G^{1/2}(V_\perp-BK)s$; use any minimizer if the system is singular. Minimize its residual sum over the planes to obtain the optimal projector whenever attained (or take the infimum). If Euclidean orthogonality is required, set $K=0$ throughout. That search attains its minimum by compactness. A free rank-$r$ encoder/decoder product need not be idempotent, so dropping the projector constraint changes the general problem.

**Proved (failure of marginal averaging).** In two dimensions take $u=\pm e_i$ with independent fair sign and uniform $i$. Given $i$, set $G=e_i e_i^\top$ in world A and $G=e_{3-i}e_{3-i}^\top$ in world B. Both have $C=\mathbb EG=I/2$. For rank one the first objective is $\tfrac12\sum_i(1-Q_{ii})^2$, minimized at equal diagonal entries since $\operatorname{tr}Q=1$. The second is $\tfrac12(Q_{12}^2+Q_{21}^2)$, minimized by a coordinate projector. These minimizer sets are disjoint even allowing oblique projectors. This is a counterexample for the general local-metric problem. Each metric can arise from a fair binary output with log-odds derivative $2e_i$ or $2e_{3-i}$. Thus even knowing *both* marginals exactly cannot select a universally optimal plane.

**Proved (spectral case).** Suppose $\mathcal K=C\otimes M$ with $M\succ0$; a constant $G=M$ suffices, as does independence of $G$ and $uu^\top$ with $M=\mathbb EG$. For any retained plane, the best reconstruction of each $u$ is its $M$-orthogonal projection onto that plane. In coordinates $y=M^{1/2}u$ the problem becomes Euclidean PCA on $\mathbb E yy^\top=M^{1/2}CM^{1/2}$. If $U_r$ contains its top orthonormal eigenvectors, one optimal solution and its discarded weight are
$$
Q_* = M^{-1/2} U_r U_r^\top M^{1/2},\qquad
\mathcal E(Q_*)=\sum_{j>r}\lambda_j(M^{1/2}CM^{1/2}).
$$
The trace argument above proves optimality. $Q_*$ is generally oblique in stream coordinates. For singular constant $M$ work on $\operatorname{ran}M$ using its restricted inverse; components along $\ker M$ are free in the output norm. If $r$ exceeds the observable dimension, complete the retained space in the kernel. With $M$ proportional to the identity one recovers ordinary PCA. For commuting $C,M$, keep common axes with the largest *products* of their eigenvalues; PCA agrees if the selected axes agree. For an imposed Euclidean projector and noncommuting metrics the displayed oblique solution generally is inadmissible.

**Proved (width across a context).** Replacing all columns by the same bottleneck gives the quadratic coefficient
$$
\mathbb E\frac1T\sum_i\left\|S(p_i)^{1/2}
\sum_{j\le i}J_{ij}(I-Q)u_j\right\|^2,\qquad J_{ij}=\partial z_i/\partial x_j
$$
for one sequence with all positions scored. This follows by the chain rule before squaring. The single-column quadratic omits cross terms between different sources. One can fit the same plane and encoder with the coupled residual in this formula. Averaging independent column scores is exact when the cross terms vanish. A bottleneck applied at multiple layers likewise has interfering first-order perturbations. Finite deletion can change attention probabilities, cross ReLU kinks and change LayerNorm statistics. The local coefficient controls the limit $t\to0$; extending it to $t=1$ needs a remainder bound along the path. A width-$r$ architecture must also implement its own LayerNorm, residual maps, head partition and positional code. A projector at one interface proves no such closure.

**Proved (LayerNorm).** Put $C_0=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/D$, $c=C_0x$, $\sigma^2=\|c\|^2/D+\epsilon$. The differential of the normalization before its gain and bias is $C_0/\sigma-cc^\top/(D\sigma^3)$, obtained by differentiating $c/\sigma$. It kills $\mathbf1$, maps $c$ to $\epsilon c/\sigma^3$, and scales directions perpendicular to both by $1/\sigma$. Left-multiply by $\operatorname{diag}\gamma$ for the affine LayerNorm. PicoGPT uses positive $\epsilon$, so the radial direction generally survives. The skip paths carry a per-token shift along $\mathbf1$ unchanged through normalized branches; the final LayerNorm removes it. Thus the entire suffix is exactly blind to such shifts, even across attention, and its Fisher is singular. The radial response of a single normalization gives no common radial null direction for a residual stack. These identities follow directly from [the block](../../src/block.jl) and [the readout](../../src/model.jl).

## 2. An area law for a tower of worlds?

**Proved (operational meaning).** On a common probability space let the finite pasts $H_k$ refine one another, $H_{k-1}=\pi_k(H_k)$, and let $Y_k$ be a specified finite future observable, for example the next fine token, or a block of them. Use the *same* target $Y_k$ on both sides of each comparison. Define new predictive information, in nats, by
$$
a_k=I(Y_k;H_k\mid H_{k-1})
=H(Y_k\mid H_{k-1})-H(Y_k\mid H_k)
=\mathbb E\,D_{\rm KL}\big(P(Y_k\mid H_k)\,\|\,P(Y_k\mid H_{k-1})\big).
$$
The last equality follows by conditioning the log ratio. Thus $\sup_k a_k\le A$ means that ignoring any one refinement costs at most $A$ in *Bayes* log loss, with the optimal coarse-history predictor for that target. It bounds neither surprise in the new output labels nor the loss of an arbitrary fitted parent. For a fixed target across a finite tower the sum telescopes to $I(Y;H_n\mid H_0)\le nA$. With changing targets it need not telescope. For a spatial area law one bounds entanglement *across a cut*; a bound per refinement permits accumulated information to grow. Even on the MPS side an entropy bound differs from a rank bound: arbitrarily many small positive Schmidt weights can carry arbitrarily little entropy. [Schuch et al., 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.0292)

**Proved (two towers).** Sample independent fair bits. Level $k$ observes the first $m_k$ as its history, and its future copies those bits. Project histories and futures by taking prefixes. Then $a_k=(m_k-m_{k-1})\log 2$. Taking $m_k=k$ satisfies the bound and the exact predictive state has $2^k$ possible values. Taking $m_k=k(k+1)/2$ violates it. These are two-time worlds and require no stationarity assumption. If the future is always one binary token, $a_k\le\log 2$ in *every* world, which shows why the choice of observable and horizon matters.

**Proved (width can grow arbitrarily in one bounded refinement).** Start with constant coarse past and future. In one refinement let the history be a uniform symbol $i$ out of $K$, and set the next-token logits to $Z_{ai}=\tau\,\mathbf1_{a=i}$ with fixed $\tau>0$. The future marginal is uniform by symmetry. Each log likelihood ratio to uniform is at most $\tau$, so $a_1\le\tau$ independently of $K$. Repeat the identity refinement thereafter. Let $B_K=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/K$. Any logits giving the same probabilities differ columnwise by a constant, hence $B_K Z B_K=\tau B_K$ for *every* exact realization. For PicoGPT logits $Z=W_{\rm out}X_f+b_{\rm out}\mathbf1^\top$, double centering bounds that rank by $D$. In fact the final LayerNorm centers into a $(D-1)$-dimensional plane before its fixed diagonal affine map, so $\operatorname{rank}(X_f B_K)\le D-1$. Thus $D\ge K$ irrespective of depth. The dense readout alone then has at least $K^2$ entries. For even $K$ the task is realizable with $T=1,L=0,D=K$: cancel the fixed positional column in $E$ and embed symbol $i$ as $e_i$. All columns have the same normalization scale $\sigma$; use unit gain, zero biases and $W_{\rm out}=\tau\sigma I$. Softmax discards the resulting common shift. This is an exact-representation lower bound; if allowed excess log loss $\tau$, the uniform predictor suffices.

**Proved (even a binary target can hide a large table).** On $N$ equiprobable histories let a binary future copy an arbitrary Boolean labeling of them. The increment from a trivial past is at most $\log 2$. To classify every possible labeling exactly, a family needs at least $2^N$ distinct input-output functions. An implementation with $b$ bits of storage per free parameter, fixed architecture and no other task-dependent storage needs at least $N/b$ parameters in the worst case, by counting. This argument counts stored bits and does not apply to unrestricted real-number encodings. Conversely, an additional sufficient statistic $S_k$ with at most $m$ values and $Y_k\perp H_k\mid(H_{k-1},S_k)$ would suffice to transmit the detail in $\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ bits. Its computation from the fine past and its decoder may still be expensive. Such a statistic or an architectural approximation theorem is additional structure beyond a bound on $a_k$.

## 3. Growth, two-site updates and sweeps

**Proved (criterion for freezing).** Let $\theta$ range over inherited parameters, $\phi$ over the increment, and $\theta_0$ be the coarse optimum actually chosen. Assume the lift at $\phi_0$ agrees with the parent on the specified coarse predictions. Define $f(\theta)=\inf_\phi L_{\rm fine}(\theta,\phi)$. Freezing attains the *joint loss value* exactly when $f(\theta_0)=\inf_\theta f(\theta)$, by taking the two infima in order. With attained minima this says that the frozen slice contains a joint minimizer. For a guarantee independent of which parent minimizer training selects, its entire minimizer set must lie in $\arg\min f$. Equality at birth says nothing about this condition after fitting $\phi$. For a strict gap some inherited parameter must move to reach the joint value. Releasing it does not guarantee a particular optimizer will find that value.

**Proved (exact split).** Let $v$ be the coarse token, $b$ its fine label, $h$ the fine history and $\pi h$ its coarse version. Suppose the child has a product parameter space and normalized factors
$$
p_{\theta,\phi}(v,b\mid h)=p_\theta(v\mid\pi h)\,q_\phi(b\mid v,h).
$$
Sum over $b$ to recover the parent at every $\phi$. On fine data the negative log likelihood is $L_c(\theta)+L_b(\phi)$ provided the parent was fitted on exactly the pushed-forward training law with the same weights. Each summand can be minimized separately; any pair of minimizers is joint even for misspecified factors. A conditional head reading a trainable parent representation generally gives $L_b(\theta,\phi)$, and a flat softmax split generally changes the coarse marginal. A factorization of probabilities alone always exists by the chain rule. The separation that matters for freezing is in the optimized parameters and the training law.

**Proved (a minimal vocabulary split with a strict frozen gap).** One history, two coarse alternatives $A,B$. Split $A$ into $+,-$ with fine law $w=(3/8,1/8,1/2)$. The parent predicts $P(A)=\operatorname{sigmoid}(a)$, uniquely optimal at $a=0$. Take flat child logits
$$
z(a,u)=(a+u-\log 2,\ a-u-\log 2,\ 0).
$$
They use only output biases, with a tied fine contrast $u$. At $u=0$ the coarse marginal is exactly the parent's for *every* $a$. Subsequently the coarse odds are $e^a\cosh u$ and the within-$A$ odds are $e^{2u}$. Joint fitting gives $u_*=\tfrac12\log3,\ a_*=-\log\cosh u_*$, which matches $w$ and attains its entropy. With $a=0$, matching the coarse odds forces $u=0$, contradicting the required fine odds. More explicitly the frozen loss is $\log(1+\cosh u)-u/4+(\log2)/2$; its derivative $\tanh(u/2)-1/4$ increases strictly through zero at $u_f=\log(5/3)$. The frozen output is $(25,9,30)/64$, and the strict excess loss is $D_{\rm KL}(w\,\|\,p_{0,u_f})$. One history is sufficient; a nonconstant coarse marginal needs at least two alternatives, and a nontrivial split needs at least three fine ones under that requirement. Minimality is for this marginal-vs-contrast obstruction. Allowing the child an independent shared offset on both split logits restores the missing degree of freedom even with $a$ frozen.

**Proved (the two-site comparison).** In mixed canonical MPS coordinates let $A$ contract the fixed left and right isometries with a free two-site tensor $s$; then $A^\dagger A=I$. Minimizing energy in this window solves the Rayleigh problem for $A^\dagger \mathcal H A$. After reshaping $s$ across the bond, its truncated SVD minimizes the *state-norm* error at the new bond dimension, by the first trace argument and isometry. Moving the window lets both sides of previously frozen bonds adapt. Even here state-norm truncation after the local energy solve need not minimize the energy at fixed rank.

**Proved (transformer comparison).** One can unfreeze two adjacent blocks and minimize their conditional loss with the rest fixed, then move the window. Exact minimization cannot increase that loss while the old parameters remain feasible. In PicoGPT the map of these two blocks contains residual additions, normalized queries and keys, softmax attention and an intervening ReLU, so its parameters do not enter as one free linear tensor in an isometric environment. Even an affine binary readout restricted to one bias has log loss $\log(1+e^z)-qz$; this is neither a quadratic loss in the bias nor a Rayleigh quotient of its affine coordinates. A locally free MPS tensor, a Rayleigh solve and an exact split back into two blocks by SVD have no general counterpart in this update. Section 1 gives a local distributional truncation criterion if a bottleneck is inserted after fitting; neither the SVD identity nor the monotonicity of an untruncated fit extends to finite deletion. Alternating windows may also stall: on $(xy-1)^2$, $(0,0)$ is a minimizer in either scalar coordinate with the other fixed, but joint loss zero is feasible. This example concerns block-coordinate optimization in general.

## 4. Does a lineage save work?

**Proved (work at matched accuracy).** Fix a final data law, an excess-loss tolerance and a rule for deciding it has been reached. Count from the start of the parent fit. If stage $k$ takes $n_k$ updates of average cost $c_k$, sweeps take $s_j$ updates of average cost $b_j$, and the two protocols have one-time costs $A_{\rm line},A_{\rm direct}$, the comparison is
$$
W_{\rm line}=A_{\rm line}+\sum_k n_kc_k+\sum_j s_jb_j,\qquad
W_{\rm direct}=A_{\rm direct}+n_{\rm direct}c_{\rm direct}.
$$
Charge data preparation, lifts, validation and cache construction or reads wherever incurred. If the frozen slice cannot reach tolerance, count the sweep or declare the target missed. Token counts alone hide different per-token costs. With dense multiplies a full PicoGPT forward on $N=BT$ columns costs to leading order $\Theta(N[VD+L(D^2+DF+TD)])$ for the readout, projections, MLPs and attention products, plus lower-order operations. This count follows from the matrix shapes. Dense pullbacks have the same leading orders. Freezing can skip weight gradients and optimizer work; one still pays to compute frozen features, and to backpropagate through any frozen suffix above a trainable layer. Reuse of cached features requires unchanged inputs, data transformations and feature parameters. The stock [training routine](../../src/train.jl) computes a full pullback. Merely zeroing a slice afterward saves none of those matrix multiplies.

**Proved (a strict saving for a specified training method).** Consider the exact product of two independent Bernoulli factors on one history, both fitted from logit zero by full-batch gradient descent. Retain the first factor at birth. Let the true success rates be $9/10$ and $3/4$ and the learning rate be $\eta=4\log 3$. For rate $q$ the population loss is $\ell_q(z)=\log(1+e^z)-qz$ with derivative $\sigma(z)-q$. After one update the second logit is $\log 3$, exactly its optimum. The first logit becomes $(8/5)\log3$, short of its optimum $\log 9$. Its second step strictly decreases its loss: $\ell_q''\le 1/4$ and $0<\eta<8$, so the quadratic upper bound gives strict descent at any nonstationary point. Choose the target to be the joint loss after two full steps. One direct step misses it. Two first-factor steps followed by one second-factor step give the *identical logits* as two dense joint steps, by separability. If evaluating/updating the factors costs $c_1,c_2>0$ additively, this costs $2c_1+c_2$ versus $2(c_1+c_2)$; the saving persists if extra lineage overhead is less than $c_2$. The example charges the entire parent fit and improves on an expressly dense direct algorithm.

**Proved (limits of the comparison).** A direct optimizer allowed to skip the converged factor matches that work; one allowed the analytical Bernoulli solution needs no gradient descent at all. A separable problem where both factors need the same number of independent updates already gives equality before lift overhead. More generally, a procedure for the final problem with access to the same worlds can execute the entire staged algorithm internally. Hence there is no strict complexity advantage over *all* direct algorithms with the same information and allowed operations. Neither the lift identity nor $a_k\le A$ bounds the update counts, cacheability or sweep cost in the accounting formula. **Conjectured, open for PicoGPT.** Some data towers may reduce total work relative to specified from-scratch training at matched final loss; the Bernoulli calculation supplies no estimate for this model.

## 5. Gauge freedom and canonical coordinates

**Proved (which coordinates?).** Distinguish a change of basis in the residual stream from one inside an attention head or an MLP. A useful precise question is which *global invertible linear* stream changes $x'=A x$ preserve the normalized features, up to a fixed linear map that an affine reader can absorb, for all $x$. Write $e=\mathbf1/\sqrt D$, $x=me+c$ with $c\perp e$, and $n(x)=c/\sqrt{\|c\|^2/D+\epsilon}$. With fixed $\epsilon>0$ the full answer to this question is
$$
A(me+c)=(\alpha m+\ell^\top c)e+O c,\qquad
\alpha\ne0,\quad O:e^\perp\to e^\perp\ \text{orthogonal}.
$$
Indeed $n(Ame)=0$ forces $Ae\parallel e$. On $e^\perp$, write $B=C_0 A|_{e^\perp}$; it is invertible there. If $n(Ax)=L n(x)$, comparison on infinitesimal centered inputs gives $L=B$ on that plane. Comparison on finite inputs then requires $\|Bc\|=\|c\|$ for all $c$. Conversely every displayed $A$ gives $n(Ax)=O n(x)$. The mean coordinate can be rescaled or sheared because it is invisible to every reader. Adding a common constant along $e$ is also invisible. A scaling of the centered stream changes the normalization denominator relative to the fixed $\epsilon$.

**Proved (implementing the gauge).** For *each* reader $W,b$ of a LayerNorm with gain $\gamma$ and bias $\beta$, fold them into $\widehat W=W\operatorname{diag}(\gamma)$ and $\widehat b=b+W\beta$. Extend $O$ by $Oe=e$. Use unit gain, zero offset and reader $\widehat W O^\top,\widehat b$ in the new coordinates. This keeps every Q, K, V, MLP preactivation and final logit identical when the streams are related by $A$. Left-multiply each branch output weight and bias by $A$ to carry the change through each residual sum. The recipe works for unequal or zero gains. It uses the fact that ReLU acts on the **hidden MLP units**, after an adjustable affine map. Demanding that a rotation commute with a fixed diagonal gain or a coordinatewise ReLU on the stream would undercount the model's reparameterizations. If only independent linear basis changes $A_\ell$ across consecutive residual cuts were allowed, the identity skips would still tie them: branch outputs are bounded on all stream inputs by normalization, bounded attention weights and fixed finite parameters. Comparing the linear growth at large $x$ forces $A_{\ell+1}=A_\ell$ for an all-input conjugacy.

**Proved (fixed positions restrict the gauge).** For literal $x'=Ax$ from the embedding onward, the new embedding would have to be $E'_a=AE_a+(A-I)P_t$ at *every* position. A single $E'_a$ works iff $(A-I)(P_t-P_1)=0$ for every allowed $t$. This stabilizer of the positional differences intersects the group just derived; even a permutation can fail it. If the differences span $\mathbb R^D$, only $A=I$ remains for literal linear conjugacy. If only predictions matter, positional discrepancies along $e$ may be allowed: $C_0(A-I)(P_t-P_1)=0$ suffices, since the residual shifts pass unread to the final LayerNorm. More generally each token embedding may be shifted independently along $e$, as may the output of either branch along $e$ (for example by adding $\mathbf1 w^\top$ to its output matrix). These are exact redundancies of the predictions. Accidental symmetries of a particular fitted model or finite dataset can be larger. The positional restriction follows from the [fixed sinusoidal code](../../src/embedding.jl).

**Proved (internal gauges).** Within each head, $q\mapsto Bq,\ k\mapsto B^{-\top}k$ with invertible $B$ leaves every score unchanged. An invertible change of value basis with its inverse in the corresponding columns of $W_o$ also cancels, and whole heads may be permuted together. A common key translation adds a row constant to the unmasked scores and leaves attention invariant. In the MLP, permutations and positive rescalings of hidden units commute with ReLU and cancel against the next matrix. An invertible map commuting with coordinatewise ReLU preserves the nonnegative cone in both directions; it must permute its extreme rays up to positive scale. Softmax also discards common logit shifts. None of these internal gauges gives an arbitrary change of residual basis. All follow from the [head scores and value mix](../../src/attention.jl) and [residual updates](../../src/block.jl).

**Proved (what can be canonicalized).** For the single-interface experiment fix the data law, mean convention, output loss, and intervention class. Under a passive coordinate change $x'=Ax$, $C'=ACA^\top$, $G'=A^{-\top}GA^{-1}$ and $Q'=AQA^{-1}$ give exactly the same quadratic error by substitution. Here the mean maps along with $x$; imposing Euclidean orthogonality anew after a shear changes the class. In the separable case, on the observable space with $M\succ0$, the coordinates $U^\top M^{1/2}(x-m)$ have identity sensitivity metric and diagonal covariance. The eigenvalue list and, at a spectral gap, the retained plane are intrinsic to this *experiment*: equivalently the retained directions are right eigenvectors of $CM$, which transforms by similarity. Signs, basis within ties, and completions in null directions remain arbitrary. These coordinates are available for analysis even if that transformation violates the network's positional or normalization constraints. Whitening alone does not turn a sample-dependent $G$ into a constant. Two unequal metrics remain unequal under invertible congruence, and the counterexample in Section 1 requires the joint moment.

**Proved (boundary of the analogy).** A mixed canonical MPS makes a local tensor's norm agree with the state's norm by an exact isometry of its environment. Here the natural local metric depends on the predicted distribution and the data, is singular even in the mean-stream direction, and may couple every source position in the causal past. The gauge above supplies no transformation that makes every such metric a common identity or makes general width deletion an exact SVD step. A well-defined truncation *criterion* exists once its experiment is specified. **Conjectured, open for PicoGPT.** At a given tolerance, a trained model may admit a much smaller executable transformer with nearly the retained predictions. Establishing that requires controlling finite perturbations, coupled positions and closure in the narrower architecture.

## Verification

**Checked numerically.** The Julia checks exercise the local LayerNorm Jacobian by centered finite differences and the weighted discarded norm against singular values. Through a causal PicoGPT block they compare the curvature of output KL under simultaneous two-column removal with the sum of the columns' logit perturbations *before* squaring. Squaring each separately gives a different value. A complete stream conjugacy is checked at the embedding, attention residual, block output and logits with the fixed positional code. Further tests cover the oblique normal equation, internal head and MLP gauges, the two-site MPS norm, the hard-width family, the split loss, the frozen optimum and the separable work example. The frozen excess on the three-outcome world is 0.002238133166642009 nats per observation. [Reproducible Julia checks](check_dmrg.jl)
