# Independent review of written and looped negation

## Corrections, in order of consequence

1. **Incorrect as written in the opening.** For fair words and $T>2$, $\ln 2$ is the **minimum population end-only cross-entropy** of one blank pass. The loss of an arbitrary predictor can be larger. If it assigns probability $q$ to `be` on a given visible context, its conditional loss is $-\tfrac12\ln(q(1-q))$. This equals $\ln 2$ only at $q=1/2$. The table states the optimum correctly. For determined labels, zero is an infimum approached as the margin grows. The witness already gives exact argmax answers at finite weights. [Reviewed note, §1](written-vs-looped.md#1-where-the-intermediate-answer-lives)
2. **Correct with an Ising sign qualification.** For fixed input and an independent binary symmetric channel on each edge, the physical spin product has mean $c_i=(-1)^{x_i}(1-2\epsilon_i)$. For $0<\epsilon_i<1$, set $\beta J_i=\operatorname{atanh}(c_i)$. The intended update is the *preferred* sign only for $\epsilon_i<1/2$. At $1/2$ neither sign is preferred, and above $1/2$ the opposite sign is preferred. Error rates zero and one require the deterministic limiting coupling. The probability formula works on the whole closed interval. [Reviewed note, §2](written-vs-looped.md#2-a-wrong-intermediate-answer)
3. **Correct with a coordinate qualification.** The magnitude bound of one in the witness proof applies to coefficients such as $a^\mathsf TX/4$ in the centered orthogonal basis. A raw feature of unit-gain LayerNorm can exceed one. For example, the marked first embedding $a+c=(2,0,0,-2)$ gives a feature $2/\sqrt{2+10^{-5}}$. The proof only uses the coefficient bound. [Witness](written-vs-looped.md#weights-for-the-270-parameter-block)

## Errors, distance and chained lookup

- **Written and loop errors — correct with the stated independence assumption.** Relative to the actual incoming state, the final error bit is the XOR of all edge flips. Its sign expectation is the product of their sign expectations. Thus $P(\mathrm{error})=(1-\prod_i(1-2\epsilon_i))/2$, giving $(1-(1-2\epsilon)^t)/2$ at equal rates. With an exact seed the written terminal decision and first arriving predecessor-only answer each traverse $T-1$ noisy updates. If the boundary is recomputed on later passes, count noisy updates from that boundary, including its emission if noisy. Rational enumeration through eight transitions verified homogeneous and heterogeneous rates. Two perfectly correlated flips always cancel, illustrating why teacher-forced accuracy alone cannot supply rollout error. The deterministic gain recurrence and a soft block with multiple paths need their own error models. [Register recurrence](audit.md#5-the-two-gain-prefix-register)
- **Distance — correct.** A layer with width $w$ includes itself and shifts the left endpoint by at most $w-1$. Applying the interval bound $KL$ times gives $KL(w-1)$ with the stated pointwise injections. A full-attention layer can read a distant address in one operation. Additional heads in that operation issue their queries before receiving values.
- **Serial lookup — correct under the specified oracle.** With only the current address, a query can follow at most $h$ links in $h$ serial reads. Consider all tables equal to the identity. Changing the first unread table to swap the current address with a second address preserves the entire observed transcript and changes the end address. Following the chain attains the lower bound $\lceil n/h\rceil$. Parallel table preprocessing and global transformer summaries are different access models. Published finite-precision bounds also constrain head width and communication precision. [One-layer theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08164v1#S3)
- **Pointer doubling — correct for binary concatenation.** A summary spans at most twice its previous maximum after a simultaneous round. Combining adjacent disjoint intervals attains the bound until the sentinel. The bit at $t$ covers $\min(t,2^r)$ inputs and the address is $(t-2^r)_+$. Independent enumeration checked both registers on all words through length eight. The abstract scan takes $\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ rounds. The cited stock implementation pays a preparation pass and uses a length-dependent construction. [Finite stock construction](derivations.md#shared-weight-pointer-doubling)

## Gate, stability and equilibrium

**Closure and the pullback — correct.** Writing $Y_k=(1-g)S+gX_{k-1}$ shows that at zero every call receives $S$, and at one it receives the previous stream. The first call is independent of $g$ for every gain. At closure the final predictor, loss and backbone derivative equal the one-pass values for the same stream normalization. A mean of identical pass losses preserves this equality. With $V_k=A_Y^\mathsf T\bar X_k$, the three derivatives of the mixer contribute $gV_k$ to the preceding state, $(1-g)V_k$ to the seed and $\langle V_k,X_{k-1}-S\rangle$ to the gain. At $k=1$, adding the initial-state cotangent to the seed gives its full $V_1$. The anchor has a separate direct derivative on every call. Summing both routes before the embedding scatter handles repeated token IDs. Channel gains sum over positions, and a tanh parameterization adds the usual $1-g_i^2$ factor. The gain-one identity is algebraic. Computing the literal subtraction and addition can round in floating point. At a ReLU kink, derivative statements use the repository's chosen backward rule or require a separate directional check. [Block pullback](../src/window.jl)

**Can it open? Correct with the stated objective and distribution.** For final loss and $K\ge2$, a gain perturbation at closure gives $\partial_gX_K=A_Y(A(S,U)-S)$. Earlier state derivatives are multiplied by zero. Hence the gate derivative can be positive, negative or zero. For $T>3$, the terminal logit direction depends on at most three suffix bits. With a uniform forecast on each observed context, the paired gold labels give opposite cross-entropy gradients. Their population mean is zero even when individual gradients are large. With mean supervision over identical pass targets, the gain derivative at closure is $(K-1)/K$ times the final-pass expression because only pass one has zero message. [Gate design](written-vs-looped.md#4-what-a-closed-gate-stabilizes)

On an independent three-word, final-pass prefix-CE example, I obtained gain derivatives 7.46708848 at $g=0$ and −0.01425710 at $g=0.17$. Central differences agreed to $8.3\times10^{-8}$ for the gain and $1.0\times10^{-10}$ for a backbone direction across the two checks. With a uniform terminal readout at length four, finite differences on all 16 words gave mean gate derivative zero and largest absolute example derivative 0.01581. The independent seed tests the reported opening signal and pullback. The source's specific seeded gradient values and finite-difference residuals remain its own measurements. [Independent check](../scratch/check-wvl/check.jl)

**Jacobian and normalization — correct.** At fixed input, $J=gA_Y$ for scalar gain and $J=A_Y(I_N\otimes\operatorname{diag}g)$ in column-major channel notation. At zero the map is constant in the old state. A uniform Lipschitz bound $L$ gives contraction when $|g|L<1$, with an invariant complete domain for a fixed-point guarantee. Fixed unit-gain stream LN bounds column norm by $\sqrt D$ but can amplify small perturbations near zero variance. Without it the anchored block still has bounded outputs for fixed parameters and anchor by bounded pre-LN values, convex attention weights and finite affine maps. An output bound alone supplies no contraction factor. Adding $\mathbf1 c^\mathsf T$ to the ordinary block input passes that translation through both residuals, whereas the anchored residual uses fixed $U$ and erases the translation. The associated Jacobian eigenvalues are therefore $1$ for the ordinary block, $g$ for its scalar restart and $0$ for the anchored block. A channelwise or input-dependent gain needs its own analysis. Whether a learned gate can replace stream LN in training remains open. [Translation proof](derivations.md#5-linearization-and-the-light-cone)

**Inkling comparison — correct.** The release describes twelve middle layers executed three times and an initially blocked message. Goodfire gives separate previous-pass mixtures for Q, K, V and MoE, two normalized source sums, and a 64-dimensional gate projection. Substituting $F=G=0$ into that formula gives $x'=x$. Differentiating at closure gives the two signed outer products in the note and zero cotangent to $z$ through this path. Setting $P=0$ as well would zero both outer products. The sources establish the functional initial condition. The choice of zero matrices is a sufficient construction. The separate variable-loop model improved on N-Hop Lookup through the measured ten passes, showed little change on the named closed-book benchmarks, and used shorter reasoning traces on the GPQA questions answered correctly at all five pass counts. These observations support the scoped wording in §3. [Release and evaluations](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) · [Goodfire architecture appendix](https://www.goodfire.com/research/interpreting-inkling-2-mini/)

**Fixed point — correct.** For a differentiable equilibrium branch, differentiating $X^*=G(X^*,a)$ and transposing the linear system gives $(I-J_*^\mathsf T)\lambda=\nabla_X\ell$ when $I-J_*$ is invertible. The full matrix Neumann series converges exactly when $\rho(J_*)<1$. Finite unrolls instead use the saved per-call derivatives. Passwise supervision, stable signs and small state steps do not imply the equilibrium assumptions. The literature distinction also holds. Huginn samples its unroll and truncates backpropagation. HRM and TRM supervise outer iterations, and TRM removes HRM's one-step fixed-point gradient approximation. [Implicit derivative](derivations.md#1-a-tied-block-and-its-pullback) · [Huginn §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05171v1) · [TRM §§2, 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04871v1)

## Shakespeare design and later experiments

**Architecture, count and depth — correct.** Closing the proposed gate evaluates $B_4B_3B_2B_1$ on Book 1's embeddings with its original final LN and head. Stored weights comprise four blocks of $4D^2+2DF+9D+F=198{,}272$ scalars, plus $2VD+2D+V=16{,}961$ in embedding and readout and one shared 128-channel gain. Total **810,177**. Three calls to the two-block core between prelude and coda give **eight block applications per token**, or 2,048 column applications at length 256. The eight-untied comparison totals 1,603,137 as stated. Ordinary cached incremental inference needs past activations for each distinct application. At exact closure the repeated core outputs agree and can be reused for forward inference. [Book 1 model](../../BRIEF.md#3-the-model-exact-semantics-the-julia-js-and-prose-all-implement-this) · [Parameter layout](../../src/parameters.jl)

**Sandwich pullback — correct.** For $Y_k=U+g\odot M_{k-1}$, each $V_k=\bar Y_k$ contributes directly to $\bar U$. It contributes $g\odot V_k$ to $\bar M_{k-1}$ and the column sum of $M_{k-1}\odot V_k$ to $\bar g$. The zero message contributes zero gain derivative at the first call. Reverse each core call through $B_3$ then $B_2$, sum gradients into their two shared parameter sets, and pull the sum of all $V_k$ through $B_1$ once. The coda and head each have one tape. An independent tiny four-block implementation gave exact closed-gate logits against `gpt` and matching backbone gradients. At closed and nonuniform open gain, separate gain and backbone directional derivatives matched central differences within $6.4\times10^{-11}$. [Independent check](../scratch/check-wvl/check.jl)

**Historical results and budgets — correct with caveats.** The recorded held-out losses are 1.5313 for Book 1, 1.654106346 at stock tied pass four and 1.625554936 at windowed pass six. The stock run recorded 75.14 minutes of wall time. The previous +4-bias study qualified 25/30 fresh seeds and timed a median 10.17 seconds for the update loop, excluding validation. The hand-set witness establishes existence separately from that training result. The four proposed experiments and their durations are plans. ## Reproduction

The review used independently assembled weights and gate wrappers around the repository's block primitives. Run the small checks from the repository root. Each invocation uses one Julia thread and one BLAS thread. The four backward checks use separate short invocations to limit compilation time.

~~~sh
script=loop/scratch/check-wvl/check.jl
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 julia -t1 -g0 -O0 --project "$script" forward
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 julia -t1 -g0 -O0 --project "$script" population
for model in toy sandwich; do
    for gate in closed open; do
        OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 julia --compile=min -t1 -g0 -O0 --project "$script" "$model" "$gate"
    done
done
~~~
