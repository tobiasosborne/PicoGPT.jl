## 1. Families of Ideas

A **looped transformer** (also called a *weight-tied*, *recursive*, or *recurrent-depth* transformer) replaces a stack of $L$ distinct transformer blocks with a smaller block $B_\theta$ (often $1$–$4$ layers, or a full stack) applied $R$ times to its own output, decoupling **executed depth** from **parameter count**. Six distinct research threads feed into the 2025–2026 literature.

### 1.1 Weight-tied and recurrent-depth transformers

* **Founding — Universal Transformers.** Dehghani, Gouws, Vinyals, Uszkoreit, & Kaiser (2018; ICLR 2019), *Universal Transformers* ([arXiv:1807.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)). Applies a single shared transformer block recurrently in parallel across all positions, with per-position Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) halting; combines the global receptive field of self-attention with recurrent inductive bias.
* **Parameter sharing for efficiency — ALBERT.** Lan, Chen, Goodman, Gimpel, Sharma, & Soricut (2019; ICLR 2020), *ALBERT: A Lite BERT for Self-supervised Learning of Language Representations* ([arXiv:1909.11942](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11942)). Cross-layer parameter sharing across all encoder layers (fixed depth, no adaptive halting) plus factorized embeddings, cutting parameter count while matching or beating BERT-large on GLUE/RACE/SQuAD.
* **Modern latent-reasoning recurrent depth — Huginn-3.5B.** Geiping, McLeish, Jain, Kirchenbauer, Singh, Bartoldson, Kailkhura, Bhatele, & Goldstein (Feb 2025), *Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach* ([arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)). Introduces the **prelude–core–coda** ("sandwich") macro-architecture: a prelude $P$ embeds tokens into $\mathbf{e} = P(\mathbf{x})$, a shared recurrent core $R$ iterates $\mathbf{s}_i = R(\mathbf{e}, \mathbf{s}_{i-1})$ from random initial state $\mathbf{s}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ for a randomly sampled loop count $r$ during training (truncated backprop), and a coda $C$ decodes $\mathbf{p} = C(\mathbf{s}_r)$. Scaled to **3.5B parameters** ($h = 5280$) over **800B tokens** (`huginn-0125`), unrolling at test time up to computation equivalent to a 50B dense model.
* **Probing Huginn.** Lu, Yang, Lee, Li, & Liu (July 2025; COLM 2025 Workshop), *Latent Chain-of-Thought? Decoding the Depth-Recurrent Transformer* ([arXiv:2507.02199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02199)). Probed `huginn-0125` via coda/logit lens to test whether intermediate states form readable latent CoT; found mixed, non-verbalized internal structure rather than clean step-by-step natural-language thoughts.
* **Architectural disentanglement (2026).** Huang, Shi, Chen, Wen, Liu, Xing, & Ma (Oct 2026), *Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part I: Topology, Input Injection, Recurrent-State Design* ([arXiv:2610.23036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23036)). At matched scale (730M params / 336B tokens; MoE 8B-A0.8B / 500B tokens), isolates three axes: the prelude–core–coda sandwich consistently helps reasoning/extraction; input injection helps knowledge retrieval and context use; random initialization and split $H/L$ states give mixed or negative returns. Overall, Huginn-style designs beat Ouro across all 10 benchmarks at matched scale.

### 1.2 Programmable computers, learning algorithms, length generalization, latent thoughts, and circuit complexity

* **Programmable computers (hand-built weights).** Giannou, Rajput, Sohn, Lee, Lee, & Papailiopoulos (2023; ICML 2023), *Looped Transformers as Programmable Computers* ([arXiv:2301.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13196)). Uses the input sequence as a punchcard (instructions + memory + scratchpad) and constructs explicit weight matrices for a shallow looped encoder to emulate a one-instruction-set computer (`SUBLEQ`, generalized to `FLEQ`): **9 layers / 2 heads** for `SUBLEQ`, **13 layers / 1 head** for matrix inversion, power iteration, and in-context SGD.
* **Graph algorithms (hand-built weights).** de Luca & Fountoulakis (Feb 2024), *Simulation of Graph Algorithms with Looped Transformers* ([arXiv:2402.01107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01107)). Constructs a looped transformer (with graph-adjacency attention heads) of **19 layers, 3 heads, $O(1)$ width** (parameter count independent of graph size) that simultaneously simulates BFS, DFS, and Dijkstra, plus a construction for Kosaraju's SCC and a constant-width Turing-completeness result; explicitly notes finite-precision limits on maximum graph size.
* **Learning iterative algorithms in context.** Yang, Lee, Nowak, & Papailiopoulos (2023; ICLR 2024), *Looped Transformers are Better at Learning Learning Algorithms* ([arXiv:2311.12424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12424)). Trains a 1-layer looped GPT-2 block with input injection $Y_{t+1} = M(Y_t + P)$ to solve in-context linear regression, sparse regression, decision trees, and 2-layer ReLU NNs, matching a 12-layer unlooped transformer with **$<10\%$ (1/12) of the parameters**.
* **Optimization theory for in-context gradient descent.** Gatmiry, Saunshi, Reddi, Jegelka, & Kumar (Oct 2024), *Can Looped Transformers Learn to Implement Multi-step Gradient Descent for In-context Learning?* ([arXiv:2410.08292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08292)). Moves from expressivity to optimization, proving gradient-based training of linear looped transformers converges to multi-step preconditioned gradient descent.
* **Length generalization by looping.** Fan, Du, Ramchandran, & Lee (Sept 2024; ICLR 2025), *Looped Transformers for Length Generalization* ([arXiv:2409.15647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15647)). Formalizes iterative tasks as $n$-RASP-L programs (requiring $T(n)$ loop steps of a fixed RASP-L block, e.g., $T(n)=n$ for parity and copy, $T(n)=n+1$ for binary addition), trains decoder-only NoPE looped blocks with input injection and step-dependent end-to-end supervision on lengths $[1, 20)$, and achieves strong length generalization where depth-matched unlooped transformers fail.
* **Latent thoughts & CoT equivalence.** Saunshi, Dikkala, Li, Kumar, & Reddi (Feb 2025; ICLR 2025), *Reasoning with Latent Thoughts: On the Power of Looped Transformers* ([arXiv:2502.17416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416)). Shows a $k$-layer transformer looped $L$ times—denoted $(k \otimes L)$—nearly matches an iso-FLOP $(kL \otimes 1)$ model and vastly beats an iso-param $(k \otimes 1)$ model on $n$-ary addition, $p$-hop induction, symbolic i-GSM, and language modeling reasoning tasks. Proves that **$T$ loops simulate $T$ steps of explicit CoT** in latent space, and identifies a **reasoning-vs.-memorization dichotomy** (looping boosts algorithmic reasoning despite higher raw perplexity than iso-FLOP untied models).
* **Expressivity, circuit complexity, and memory-budget separations:**
  * *Fixed-depth barriers:* Hahn (2019; TACL 2020), *Theoretical Limitations of Self-Attention in Neural Sequence Models* ([arXiv:1906.06755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06755)) — fixed-depth hard/soft attention cannot compute parity or Dyck languages at arbitrary length. Merrill, Sabharwal, & Smith (2021; TACL 2022) ([arXiv:2106.16213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.16213)) and Merrill & Sabharwal (2022; TACL 2023), *The Parallelism Tradeoff* ([arXiv:2207.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729)) — constant-depth log-precision transformers lie inside uniform $\mathsf{TC}^0$, hence cannot solve $\mathsf{NC}^1$-complete state tracking (e.g., $S_5$ word problem) unless $\mathsf{TC}^0 = \mathsf{NC}^1$. Merrill, Petty, & Sabharwal (2024; ICML 2024), *The Illusion of State in State-Space Models* ([arXiv:2404.08819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819)) — linear SSMs (S4/Mamba) are also bounded by $\mathsf{TC}^0$.
  * *One-layer parity lower bound (2026):* Hsu (May 2026), *Lower bounds for one-layer transformers that compute parity* ([arXiv:2605.12171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12171)) — proves a single self-attention layer post-processed by a rational function cannot sign-represent $n$-bit parity unless $(\text{heads}) \times (\text{degree}) = \Omega(n)$, with a margin-dependent extension to ReLU MLPs.
  * *Shortcuts vs. growing depth:* Liu, Ash, Goel, Krishnamurthy, & Zhang (2022; ICLR 2023), *Transformers Learn Shortcuts to Automata* ([arXiv:2210.10749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10749)) — $O(\log T)$ depth suffices for any finite-state automaton via parallel prefix / Krohn–Rhodes shortcuts, though shortcuts can be brittle out-of-distribution. Merrill & Sabharwal (Mar 2025; NeurIPS 2025), *A Little Depth Goes a Long Way: The Expressive Power of Log-Depth Transformers* ([arXiv:2503.03961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03961)) — proves $\Theta(\log n)$ depth (which a looped block supplies via $\Theta(\log n)$ passes) breaks out of $\mathsf{TC}^0$ to express regular-language state tracking and graph connectivity, and matches empirical training depth requirements.
  * *Parity via one-layer recursive CoT:* Kim & Suzuki (Oct 2024; ICLR 2025 Oral), *Transformers Provably Solve Parity Efficiently with Chain of Thought* ([arXiv:2410.08633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08633)) — proves a 1-layer transformer trained by gradient descent solves $k$-parity efficiently with intermediate recursive steps where non-recursive training fails.
  * *Memory-budget separation (2026):* Zhang (May 2026), *Chain-of-Thought and Compressed Looped Transformers: A Memory-Budget Separation* ([arXiv:2605.30757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30757)) — distinguishes **compressed latent loops** ($s$ mutable latent slots, workspace $O(sdp + \log n)$, cannot decide P-complete problems under standard assumptions) from **full sequence-state loops** (mutable state at every position $n$, memory-rich like CoT), validated on pointer-chasing and associative-recall sweeps.
  * *Recurrent Looped Transformer for state tracking (2026):* Zhang, Feng, Qin, Kung, & Wang (Oct 2026), *Recurrent Looped Transformer* ([arXiv:2610.07591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07591)) — splits layers into a parallel causal encoder and a token-recurrent decoder; trained on $\le 40$ bits, generalizes parity to 256 bits (100%) and swap-$S_5$ to $8\times$ training length (97% vs. $<1\%$ for a depth-matched standard transformer).

### 1.3 Equilibrium models (DEQs) and implicit differentiation

* **Founding — Deep Equilibrium Models.** Bai, Kolter, & Koltun (2019; NeurIPS 2019 Spotlight Oral), *Deep Equilibrium Models* ([arXiv:1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377)). Treats a weight-tied infinite-depth network as solving the fixed-point equation $z^\star = f_\theta(z^\star, x)$ directly via root-finding (Broyden/Anderson), and backpropagates via the implicit function theorem—requiring solving $(I - J_{f_\theta}(z^\star)^\top)^{-1} g$ in **$O(1)$ memory** independent of depth. Demonstrated on self-attention transformers (WikiText-103) with up to 88% training-memory reduction.
* **Path independence and upwards generalization.** Anil, Pokle, Liang, Treutlein, Wu, Bai, Kolter, & Grosse (2022; NeurIPS 2022), *Path Independent Equilibrium Models Can Better Exploit Test-Time Computation* ([arXiv:2211.09961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961)). Shows that a recurrent/DEQ model's ability to extrapolate to harder test problems with more iterations correlates strongly with **path independence**: converging to the same fixed point regardless of initial state $z_0$.
* **Recurrent algorithm synthesis ("DeepThinking").** Schwarzschild et al. (2021; NeurIPS 2021) ([arXiv:2106.04537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04537)) and Bansal, Schwarzschild, Borgnia, Emam, Huang, Goldblum, & Goldstein (2022; NeurIPS 2022), *End-to-end Algorithm Synthesis with Recurrent Networks: Logical Extrapolation Without Overthinking* ([arXiv:2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826)). Although convolutional/residual rather than transformer-based, these directly seeded Huginn and modern looped transformers by introducing **recall (input injection)** and **progressive/random-depth training** to eliminate *overthinking* and converge to fixed points over thousands of iterations (prefix sums, mazes, chess).

### 1.4 Small recursive reasoning models: HRM, TRM, and their debates (2025–2026)

* **Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM).** Wang, Li, Sun, Chen, Liu, Wu et al. (Sapient Intelligence, June 2025), *Hierarchical Reasoning Model* ([arXiv:2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734)). A **27M-parameter** randomly initialized model with two coupled recurrent modules—slow planner $f_H$ and fast worker $f_L$—trained with **1-step implicit gradient** (invoking DEQ fixed-point intuition), **deep supervision** across outer refinement steps, and ACT halting. Trained from scratch on ~1,000 examples per task (plus data augmentation), it achieved **55.0%** on Sudoku-Extreme, **74.5%** on Maze-Hard, and **40.3%** on ARC-AGI-1 (~5% on ARC-AGI-2), beating large CoT LLMs (e.g., o3-mini-high, Claude 3.7) on those puzzle sets.
* **ARC Prize Foundation audit (Aug 2025).** ARC Prize Team (15 Aug 2025), *The Hidden Drivers of HRM's Performance on ARC-AGI* ([arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)). Independently reproduced HRM on hidden Semi-Private sets (**32%** ARC-AGI-1, **2%** ARC-AGI-2), but ablations punctured the biological-hierarchy story:
  1. The outer **deep-supervision refinement loop** doubled accuracy (**19% → 39%**), whereas the $H/L$ hierarchy itself contributed only a small gain over a regular transformer (**35.7% → 39.0%**).
  2. Most ARC performance came from **test-time / pre-training task augmentation** (~300 augmentations needed) and per-task memorization rather than general cross-task transfer.
* **Tiny Recursive Model (TRM).** Jolicoeur-Martineau (Samsung SAIL Montréal, Oct 2025), *Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks* ([arXiv:2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)). Stripped away HRM's biological framing, 1-step fixed-point approximation, and dual $f_H/f_L$ modules, leaving a **single 2-layer network** that recursively updates a latent state $z$ ($n$ steps) and an answer embedding $y$ ($T$ cycles) with deep supervision and EMA.
  * On Sudoku-Extreme (1k train / 423k test): a **5M-parameter MLP-Mixer TRM** ($T=3, n=6$, effective depth 42) jumped from HRM's **55.0% to 87.4%**; a **7M self-attention TRM** scored **74.7%** on Sudoku-Extreme, **85.3%** on Maze-Hard, **44.6%** (~45%) on ARC-AGI-1, and **7.8%** (~8%) on ARC-AGI-2.
  * Key ablations (Table 1): replacing full backprop through recursion with HRM's 1-step gradient crashed accuracy from **87.4% to 56.5%**; splitting into separate $f_H, f_L$ (10M params) *hurt* accuracy (**82.4%**); removing EMA dropped accuracy to **79.9%**.
* **2026 follow-ups:**
  * *Probabilistic TRM (PTRM):* Sghaier, Parviz, & Jolicoeur-Martineau (May 2026) ([arXiv:2605.19943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19943)) showed deterministic TRMs get trapped in suboptimal attractors; injecting Gaussian noise at deep steps and selecting trajectories with TRM's existing $Q$-head boosted Sudoku-Extreme from **87.4% to 98.75%** and Pencil Puzzle Bench from **62.6% to 91.2%** (still 7M params, no retraining).
  * *Fixed-Point Reasoners (FPRM):* Movahedi et al. (June 2026) ([arXiv:2606.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18206)) replaced HRM/TRM's nested hierarchy and learned $Q$-halting with a flat pre-norm + residual-scaled looped transformer (**7M params**) that halts directly on **fixed-point convergence**, outperforming 7M baselines on Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, ARC-AGI-1, and $A_5/S_5$ state tracking.
* **The core debate:** Critics noted apples-to-oranges comparisons with general-purpose LLMs (HRM/TRM are specialized non-autoregressive grid encoders trained from scratch on augmented puzzle sets), while supporters pointed out that tiny weight-tied iterative loops demonstrably outperform massive shallow/CoT models on structured constraint satisfaction and state tracking.

### 1.5 Looped LLMs at scale (2025–2026) and adaptive halting

* **ByteDance Ouro (Oct 2025).** Zhu, Wang, Hua, Zhang, Li, Que et al. (ByteDance Seed + UCSC/Princeton/Mila, Oct 2025), *Scaling Latent Reasoning via Looped Language Models* ([arXiv:2510.25741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)). Open-sourced **Ouro-1.4B** (24 layers, $d=2048$) and **Ouro-2.6B** (48 layers, $d=2048$), looped $R=4$ times with no prelude/coda or input injection, pretrained on **7.7T tokens**. Matches dense SOTA models up to 12B on reasoning benchmarks; controlled "physics-of-LMs" experiments showed the gain comes from **knowledge manipulation**, not increased raw memorization capacity.
* **Nanbeige4.2-3B (July 2026).** Nanbeige Lab / Yang, Huang, Lan, Chen et al. (July 2026), *Nanbeige4.2-3B: Unlocking Agentic Capabilities in a Compact Model* ([arXiv:2607.22083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22083); architectural breakdown in Raschka, Sept 2026, [magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and)). A 3B non-embedding parameter agentic LLM pretrained from scratch on **28T tokens** that loops a 22-layer stack **twice** (44 block applications) with separate per-pass KV caches (sharing KV caches hurt performance), beating Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma4-12B on agentic benchmarks.
* **2026 scaling laws, stability, and practical recipes for LoopLMs:**
  * *Iso-depth scaling law (NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight):* Schwethelm, Rueckert, & Kaissis (Apr 2026), *How Much Is One Recurrence Worth?* ([arXiv:2604.21106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21106)). Sweeps $r \in \{1,2,4,8\}$ over $\sim 50\times$ compute and fits $L = E + A(N_{\text{once}} + r^\varphi N_{\text{rec}})^{-\alpha} + B D^{-\beta}$, measuring **$\varphi = 0.46$** (i.e., looping a block $r$ times buys roughly $r^{0.46}\times$ equivalent unique parameters in validation loss—sublinear, roughly square-root).
  * *Parcae:* Prairie, Novack, Berg-Kirkpatrick, & Fu (Apr 2026) ([arXiv:2604.12946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12946)) models looping as a dynamical system to remove residual explosion and loss spikes without sandwich-norm workarounds.
  * *Loop-$\mu$P:* Wang et al. (June/Oct 2026) ([arXiv:2606.18524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18524)) derives hyperparameter-transfer residual scaling **$c = a\, N^{-1} L^{-1/2}$** for $L$ untied layers looped $N$ times.
  * *Practical training & retrofitting:* Marchenko et al. (Sept 2026), *Closing the Loop* ([arXiv:2610.00673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00673)) cuts from-scratch LoopLM pretraining from 7.7T to ~310B tokens and retrofits pretrained dense models via one learned input-mixing scalar and smoothed exit loss.
  * *Looped MoE:* Chen, Goyal, & Krishnamoorthi (Sept 2026), *Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts* ([arXiv:2609.40316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316)) and He et al. (Oct 2026), *Looping Beyond Twice (LOOM)* ([arXiv:2610.01153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01153)) address variance explosion and expert collapse to scale looped MoEs from 2 up to 9–12 loops.
  * *LoRA adaptation:* Zhu et al. (Sept 2026), *Loop Dropout* ([arXiv:2609.34218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34218)) uncovers a late-loop bias in standard LoRA on LoopLMs and fixes it with stochastic adapter masking + inverse-survival rescaling.
* **Adaptive computation and halting (evolution from 2016 to 2026):**
  1. **ACT** — Graves (2016) ([arXiv:1603.08983](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983)): cumulative halting probabilities $h_t$ stop once $\sum h_t \ge 1 - \epsilon$, penalized by a ponder cost; deterministic and differentiable, but prone to collapse or instability.
  2. **PonderNet** — Banino, Balaguer, & Blundell (2021) ([arXiv:2107.05407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407)): models halting as a stochastic geometric process regularized via KL divergence to a geometric prior; produces unbiased gradients and strong extrapolation.
  3. **Huginn (Feb 2025)** ([arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)): no learned halting head during training; samples $r$ from a Poisson–log-normal prior, and at test time halts when consecutive-step output distributions stabilize (KL divergence below threshold).
  4. **Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR)** — Bae et al. (July 2025) ([arXiv:2507.10524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524)): lightweight token-level routers dynamically assign recursion depth per token (135M–1.7B scale) and restrict attention & KV caching to still-active tokens.
  5. **Ouro (Oct 2025)** ([arXiv:2510.25741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)): learned per-step exit gate $\lambda_t(x) = \sigma(\text{Linear}_\phi(h^{(t)}))$, survival $S_t = \prod_{j=1}^t (1-\lambda_j)$, exit probability $\tilde p_t = \lambda_t S_{t-1}$, trained with entropy regularization toward a uniform prior before a compute-allocation stage.
  6. **FPRM (June 2026)** ([arXiv:2606.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18206)) & **Huang et al. Part II (Oct 2026)** ([arXiv:2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)): halt directly on **fixed-point convergence** ($\|s_{t} - s_{t-1}\| < \varepsilon$) or learn the depth prior from prediction feedback.

## 2. Central vs. Fashionable — and What the Owner Most Probably Heard About

### Central (durable mathematical & engineering core)
1. **Depth vs. parameter decoupling & $\mathsf{TC}^0$ escape:** Fixed-depth transformers cannot track non-solvable state ($S_5$) or arbitrary-length parity ([arXiv:2207.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729), [arXiv:2605.12171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12171)); looping gives $\Theta(\log n)$ or $\Theta(n)$ executed depth with $O(1)$ parameters ([arXiv:2503.03961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03961), [arXiv:2409.15647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15647), [arXiv:2502.17416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416)).
2. **Dynamical-systems stability & fixed-point convergence:** Input injection ("recall"), proper residual scaling ($N^{-1}$ in Loop-$\mu$P), and path independence separate models that extrapolate indefinitely from models that diverge or overthink ([arXiv:1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377), [arXiv:2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826), [arXiv:2211.09961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961), [arXiv:2606.18524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18524), [arXiv:2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)).
3. **Sublinear parameter-equivalence scaling & the reasoning/memorization split:** Looping buys algorithmic/compositional reasoning ($\varphi \approx 0.46$ equivalent unique parameters in validation loss, [arXiv:2604.21106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21106)) and knowledge manipulation ([arXiv:2510.25741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741)), not free factual memorization or free KV-cache savings.

### Fashionable (high noise, contested, or already superseded)
* **Brain-inspired multi-timescale $H/L$ hierarchies (HRM):** Ablated away within 4 months by ARC Prize ([arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)), TRM ([arXiv:2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)), FPRM ([arXiv:2606.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18206)), and Huang et al. Part I ([arXiv:2610.23036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23036)).
* **"Latent CoT replaces / hides explicit CoT":** Overstated. Probing Huginn showed no clean verbalized latent CoT ([arXiv:2507.02199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02199)); compressed latent loops have strict space bounds weaker than polynomial CoT ([arXiv:2605.30757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30757)); Raschka ([magazine.sebastianraschka.com](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and)) noted Astra still outputs CoT.
* **Naive depth-truncation plots:** Shown in Sept 2026 ([arXiv:2609.19934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19934)) to conflate genuine depth utilization with pushing the readout head off-distribution.

Most recent and loudest single trigger.

4. **June–October 2025 (and May 2026 PTRM) — HRM (27M) & TRM (5M/7M) on Sudoku/ARC-AGI** ([arXiv:2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734), [arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis), [arXiv:2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871), [arXiv:2605.19943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19943)) — and earlier in **Feb 2025**, **Huginn-3.5B** ([arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)), which kicked off the 2025 latent-reasoning wave.

### 3.2 Toy tasks proving a loop is *necessary* (theorems and controls)

1. **Parity & Prefix Parity:**
   * *Theorem:* Fixed-depth hard/soft-attention transformers cannot compute $n$-bit parity for arbitrary $n$ (Hahn 2019/2020, [arXiv:1906.06755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06755)). For a **single layer**, Hsu (May 2026, [arXiv:2605.12171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12171)) proves that one attention layer + rational (or ReLU) post-processing requires $(\text{heads})\times(\text{degree}) = \Omega(n)$ to sign-represent parity.
   * *Control:* In Fan et al. ([arXiv:2409.15647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15647)), a 1-layer looped transformer solves $n$-bit parity via an $n$-RASP-L loop ($T(n)=n$) and length-generalizes past $n=20$, whereas a depth-matched unlooped baseline fails beyond training length. In Zhang et al. ([arXiv:2610.07591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07591)), an 8-layer RLT trained on $\le 40$ bits hits **100%** at 256 bits while the 8-layer standard transformer stays at **chance** (50%), and ablating recurrent feedback drops RLT back to chance.
2. **Non-Abelian State Tracking ($S_5$ / $A_5$ permutation composition):**
   * *Theorem:* Constant-depth log-precision transformers (and linear SSMs) lie in $\mathsf{TC}^0$, which cannot solve $S_5$ word composition unless $\mathsf{TC}^0 = \mathsf{NC}^1$ ([arXiv:2207.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729), [arXiv:2404.08819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819)); $\Theta(\log n)$ depth breaks this barrier ([arXiv:2503.03961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03961)).
   * *Control:* Zhang et al. ([arXiv:2610.07591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07591)): at $8\times$ training length on swap-$S_5$, RLT reaches **97%** vs. **$<1\%$** for the unlooped transformer; removing feedback drops it to chance. Also tested by FPRM ([arXiv:2606.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18206)).
3. **$p$-Hop Induction / Pointer Chasing:**
   * *Theorem & Control:* In Saunshi et al. ([arXiv:2502.17416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416)), $p$-hop induction on $n=256$ tokens ($p=16, 32$) requires depth $\Omega(\log p)$ (via pointer-doubling) or $O(p)$ sequentially: a 1-layer unlooped model $(1\otimes 1)$ gets **48.9% / 49.0%**, whereas the exact same 1-layer block looped 6 times $(1\otimes 6)$ gets **99.9% / 99.5%**, matching a 6-layer untied model $(6\otimes 1)$ (99.9% / 99.6%). Zhang ([arXiv:2605.30757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30757)) uses pointer chasing to separate compressed-slot loops from full-sequence loops.
4. **Cellular Automata (Rule 30):**
   * *Control:* Kavuncu & Hafez ([arXiv:2609.19521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19521)) use Rule 30 (exact ground truth at every pass $k$) to separate depth extrapolation across loop memory designs: trained to depth 12, LSTM-UT achieves **99.7%** exact-row accuracy at depth 60 (and $>99.95\%$ at depth 1,000 for one checkpoint), whereas a standard Block UT drops to **0%** exact-row accuracy at depth 60.
5. **Sudoku / Mazes (Backtracking & Constraint Propagation):**
   * *Control:* HRM ([arXiv:2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734), Fig. 2) shows increasing transformer width yields zero gain on Sudoku-Extreme, whereas increasing effective depth via recursion unlocks accuracy; TRM ([arXiv:2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)) confirms depth scaling ($T=2, n=2$ depth 12: 73.7% vs. $T=3, n=6$ depth 42: 87.4%).

## 4. Mechanisms Inside Looped Models

1. **Fixed points and cyclic trajectories.**
   * In a single-block loop with input injection, converged states approach a fixed point $s^\star = B_\theta(s^\star, e)$ (DEQ, [arXiv:1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377); Yang et al., [arXiv:2311.12424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12424); Bansal et al., [arXiv:2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826)).
   * When an $L$-layer stack ($L>1$) is looped cyclically (Huginn, Ouro, Parcae), Blayney et al. (Apr 2026; NeurIPS 2026, [arXiv:2604.11791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11791)) find that **each layer $\ell$ in the stack converges to its own distinct fixed point $s_\ell^\star$**, so the whole block traces a stable **limit cycle** (consistent cyclic trajectory) in latent space; attention heads stabilize and replay feedforward-like "stages of inference" on every cycle.
   * Geiping et al. ([arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)) observe context-dependent dynamics in Huginn-3.5B—fast contraction on factual recall vs. structured **orbiting** on numerical/reasoning prompts.
   * Huang et al. Part II ([arXiv:2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)) exploit near-fixed-point convergence operationally: once states are close to $s^\star$, the path taken matters little, enabling truncated backprop, **terminal KV-cache sharing** ($3\times$ smaller KV cache with negligible loss), $1.79\times$ faster distilled prefill, and $2\times$ faster RL updates.
   * Wang & Reid ([arXiv:2609.01924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01924)) probe Ouro-2.6B and Huginn-0125 through the **Jacobian lens** to test whether the mid-depth "global workspace" of dense transformers survives recurrence.
2. **Overthinking (accuracy dropping with excess passes).**
   * Named by Bansal et al. ([arXiv:2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826)): recurrent models trained at fixed depth deteriorate when run past their training loop count.
   * Observed again in 2026 implicit multi-hop reasoning (Kohli et al., COLM 2026, [arXiv:2604.07822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07822)): scaling inference recurrence unlocks 5-hop $\to$ 10-hop extrapolation (after a 3-stage grokking transition: memorization $\to$ in-distribution $\to$ systematic generalization), but excessive recurrence degrades predictions.
   * Deterministic attractors can also trap states prematurely in *wrong* basins, motivating stochastic exploration in PTRM ([arXiv:2605.19943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19943)).
3. **Stability tricks and why they work.**
   * **Input injection ("recall"):** Re-feeding $\mathbf{e} = \text{Embed}(\mathbf{x})$ at every pass ($s_{k+1} = B_\theta(s_k + \mathbf{e})$ or via an adapter) prevents forgetting and divergence past training depth (Bansal et al. [arXiv:2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826); Yang et al. [arXiv:2311.12424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12424), Fig. 3; Fan et al. [arXiv:2409.15647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15647); Geiping et al. [arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171); Huang et al. Part I [arXiv:2610.23036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23036)). Huang et al. Part II ([arXiv:2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)) refine this to **orthogonal injection** (projecting out the component of $s_k$ parallel to $\mathbf{e}$ so the state neither amplifies nor cancels the injected input).
   * **Normalization & residual scaling:** Huginn used sandwich Pre+Post-Norm to arrest norm growth ([arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)); FPRM uses Pre-Norm + learnable residual scaling ([arXiv:2606.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18206)); Parcae stabilizes via dynamical-system negative-diagonal parameterization ([arXiv:2604.12946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12946)); **Loop-$\mu$P** ([arXiv:2606.18524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18524)) proves the sharp scaling law $c = a N^{-1} L^{-1/2}$: untied layers add incoherently ($\sim L^{-1/2}$, random walk) while weight-tied loops add coherently ($\sim N^{-1}$, Euler discretization of a continuous ODE).
   * **Path independence & randomized loop depth:** Initializing from random $s_0$ and training with variable/randomized loop counts (progressive training in Bansal et al., Poisson–log-normal in Huginn, prediction-feedback prior in Huang et al. Part II) forces $B_\theta$ toward a contractive, path-independent fixed-point map ([arXiv:2211.09961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09961)), eliminating overthinking and enabling extrapolation to more passes than trained.
   * **Bounded gated state for long extrapolation:** Kavuncu & Hafez ([arXiv:2609.19521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19521)) show that caching full KV history across steps (CoTFormer) actually *hurts* Rule 30 extrapolation because stale history pulls corrected states back into failure, whereas a tiny bounded LSTM-style gate lets a 20k–47k-param model extrapolate $5\times$–$80\times$ beyond training depth.

## 5. What This Means for a Tiny Specimen (~1,000 Parameters)

For a third PicoGPT book aimed at BSc physics and mathematics students—where the entire looped block $B_\theta$ must fit in $\sim 1{,}000$ parameters and be inspectable matrix by matrix—the literature gives four sharp design lessons:

1. **Best toy tasks (provably load-bearing loop, visible per pass, clean math):**
   * **Prefix parity** (and its non-abelian extension, $S_3$/$Z_4$ state tracking) is the gold-standard control: Hsu's 2026 lower bound ([arXiv:2605.12171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12171)) and Hahn's / Merrill–Sabharwal's $\mathsf{TC}^0$ theorems ([arXiv:1906.06755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06755), [arXiv:2207.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729)) give an exact mathematical reason why a 1-pass model fails as length grows, while Fan et al. ([arXiv:2409.15647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15647)) and Merrill–Sabharwal ([arXiv:2503.03961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03961)) show a 1-layer looped block succeeds with linear ($R=n$) or log-depth ($R=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ pointer-doubling) passes—and watching the accuracy front advance with $k$ immediately distinguishes linear wave propagation from $\log_2$-depth doubling.
   * **Causal elementary cellular automaton (e.g., Rule 60 / Rule 30)** pairs cleanly with prefix parity: Kavuncu & Hafez ([arXiv:2609.19521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19521)) proved CA extrapolation works at tiny widths ($d=32$, 20k params for chaotic Rule 30); linear Rule 60 (left-neighbor XOR, autonomous without input injection) is an even smaller, analytically exact cousin where every pass $k$ is directly visible as a spacetime row.
   * **$p$-hop pointer chasing** ([arXiv:2502.17416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17416), [arXiv:2605.30757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30757)) is the cleanest non-CA alternative: 1-layer unlooped fails sharply (49% vs. 99.9% for $(1\otimes 6)$ in Saunshi et al.), and each pass visibly executes one composition hop (or pointer-doubling step).
   * **Avoid Sudoku / ARC-AGI as the primary specimen:** Even the most stripped-down recursive solvers (TRM 5M–7M, FPRM 7M) need millions of parameters, 2D grids, deep supervision, and heavy data augmentation; they belong in an epilogue linking the book to the 2025–2026 hype, not as the 1,000-parameter specimen.
2. **Best architectural mechanisms to exhibit (and contrast):**
   * **With vs. without input injection:** Yang et al. ([arXiv:2311.12424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12424)), Bansal et al. ([arXiv:2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826)), and Huang et al. ([arXiv:2610.23036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23036), [arXiv:2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833)) make input injection the single most decisive dynamical switch: with injection ($s_{k+1} = B_\theta(s_k + e)$), the loop is a driven fixed-point solver (prefix parity, convergence, path independence); without injection ($s_{k+1} = B_\theta(s_k)$), it is an autonomous dynamical system (Rule 60 CA, orbits, or overthinking drift past training depth).
   * **Randomized loop count during training** (Huginn / Bansal et al.) + **$1/N$ residual scaling** (Loop-$\mu$P, [arXiv:2606.18524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18524)) give physicists an immediate bridge to ODE integration, Jacobian spectra, fixed points, and DEQ implicit differentiation $(I - J^\top)^{-1}$ ([arXiv:1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377)).
   * **Skip learned halting heads / dual $H/L$ hierarchies** in the core specimen: fixed-point halting ($\|s_k - s_{k-1}\| < \varepsilon$, [arXiv:2606.18206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18206)) or output-KL stability ([arXiv:2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)) requires zero extra parameters and directly visualizes convergence.
